## Water, Sanitation, and Emergency Disinfection

| Resource | Source | Link/Notes |
|----------|--------|------------|
| How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency | CDC | [cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html) – Current emergency boiling and 5%-9% bleach-disinfection table |
| Drinking Water Advisories | CDC | [cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/drinking-water-advisories-an-overview.html](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/drinking-water-advisories-an-overview.html) – Distinguishes boil-water, do-not-drink, and do-not-use advisories |
| Household Water Treatment | CDC | [cdc.gov/global-water-sanitation-hygiene/about/about-household-water-treatment.html](https://www.cdc.gov/global-water-sanitation-hygiene/about/about-household-water-treatment.html) – Treatment limits, including chemical/radiological contamination |
| Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water | U.S. EPA | [epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water) – Concentration-specific bleach table and emergency treatment |
| Water Treatment | American Red Cross | [redcross.org/water-treatment](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/water-safety/water-treatment.html) – Secondary authoritative source; currently uses a higher 16-drops/gallon rule for 5.25%-6% bleach than CDC/EPA use for clear water |

**Safety-critical source note:** Current authoritative sources are not always numerically identical. The water guide records material divergences rather than silently merging them. For generated/compiled artifacts, preserve the selected source, source date/review date, concentration/conditions, and any known authoritative disagreement.
