# Tsunami Preparedness Guide

This guide provides actionable steps for what to do before, during, and after a tsunami.

## Before a Tsunami: Preparation

*   [ ] **Know Your Zone:** If you live, work, or visit coastal areas, know if you are in a tsunami hazard zone.
*   [ ] **Evacuation Routes:** Learn the quickest route to high ground or inland. Practice the route.
*   [ ] **Emergency Kit:** Water, food, first aid, flashlight, batteries, whistle, important documents in waterproof bag.
*   [ ] **Natural Warnings:** A strong or long earthquake, a sudden drop or rise in ocean level, or a loud ocean roar can indicate an approaching tsunami.
*   [ ] **Sign Up for Alerts:** Tsunami Warning System, local emergency notifications.
*   [ ] **See also:** [Ready.gov Tsunami](https://www.ready.gov/tsunamis), [NOAA Tsunami](https://www.weather.gov/tsunami).

## During a Tsunami: Evacuate Immediately

*   **If you are in a tsunami hazard zone and feel a strong earthquake:**
    *   Drop, cover, and hold on. Then move to high ground or inland immediately.
    *   Do not wait for an official warning.
*   **If you hear a tsunami warning:**
    *   Evacuate at once. Follow evacuation routes. Go on foot if possible—roads may be clogged.
*   **If you are at the beach and the water recedes unusually:**
    *   This is a natural warning. Move to high ground immediately.
*   **Stay away from the coast** until officials announce the all-clear. Tsunamis can involve multiple waves over hours.

## After a Tsunami: Stay Safe

*   [ ] **Return only when officials say it is safe.**
*   [ ] **Avoid floodwaters**—they may be contaminated or hide dangerous debris.
*   [ ] **Watch for downed power lines and gas leaks.**
*   [ ] **Document damage** for insurance claims.
*   [ ] **See also:** [FEMA Tsunami](https://www.fema.gov/disaster/tsunami).
