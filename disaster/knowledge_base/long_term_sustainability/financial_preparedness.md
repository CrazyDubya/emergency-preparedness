# Financial Preparedness Guide

This guide helps you build financial resilience for emergencies and extended disruptions.

## Emergency Fund Basics

*   **Goal:** 3–6 months of essential expenses in a liquid account (savings, money market).
*   **Where:** FDIC-insured account, accessible without penalty.
*   **What counts as essential:** Rent/mortgage, utilities, food, medications, insurance.

## Emergency Budget Priorities

1. **Shelter and utilities**
2. **Food and water**
3. **Medications and medical**
4. **Insurance (health, home/renters)**
5. **Communication (phone/internet if critical)**
6. **Transportation (for work or evacuation)**

## Cash Reserve

*   Keep a small amount of cash at home for power outages or when ATMs/banks are unavailable.
*   Consider small denominations for easier use during local disruptions.

## Documentation

*   Keep copies of important documents in a safe, portable location (fireproof safe, safe deposit box, or digital backup).
*   Include: IDs, insurance policies, property deeds, bank/account info, medical records, contact list.

## Insurance Review

*   **Homeowners/Renters:** Verify coverage for disasters relevant to your area (flood, earthquake, etc.). Flood insurance is separate.
*   **Health:** Understand deductibles, out-of-network care, and prescription coverage.
*   **Auto:** Check comprehensive coverage for natural disasters.

## Debt and Expenses

*   Reduce nonessential debt to improve cash flow during emergencies.
*   Know your options for forbearance or payment relief with lenders, insurers, and utilities.

## Income Continuity

*   Understand sick leave, remote work options, and unemployment eligibility.
*   Have a backup plan if your primary income is disrupted.
