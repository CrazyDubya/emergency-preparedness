EMERGENCY FIELD KNOWLEDGE - 100 KiB BUDGET

PURPOSE
For ordinary people facing prolonged failure of normal services. If reliable emergency services, evacuation orders, public-health guidance, utility instructions, poison control, or professional medical care exist, use them. This file emphasizes actions that are broadly safe under uncertainty. A precise instruction that is wrong for your situation can be worse than a general rule.

HOW TO USE
Stop immediate hazards first. Then: severe injury/breathing; safe water; fire/CO; temperature; sanitation; people/accountability; food; communications; power; recovery. Search plain text for: BLEEDING, CPR, WATER, DIARRHEA, FIRE, CO, COLD, HEAT, FOOD, TOILET, POWER, FLOOD, EARTHQUAKE, RADIO, EVACUATION, MEDICINE, REPAIR, GARDEN.

============================================================
01 IMMEDIATE PRIORITIES
============================================================

Do not become the next casualty. Look before touching, entering, climbing, wading, driving, switching, digging, or rescuing.

Leave fire, dense smoke, strong gas/chemical fumes, rapidly rising floodwater, obvious structural collapse, active violence, or an ordered evacuation. Property is replaceable. Close doors behind you during fire if safe. Once out of a burning building, stay out.

Treat downed electrical lines as live. Stay far away from lines, wet ground around them, fences/rails touching them, and water that may be energized. Do not enter standing water to reach an electrical panel. If a line contacts a vehicle, occupants generally remain inside unless fire forces exit; avoid simultaneous contact with vehicle and ground.

Suspected gas leak: do not light flames, smoke, operate switches, start engines, or deliberately create sparks. Leave and contact the utility/fire service from a safe location if available.

Floodwater hides holes, debris, sewage, chemicals, and electricity. Do not drive, walk, or swim through moving floodwater merely because another vehicle crossed.

Count people. Identify missing/injured persons and people with urgent dependencies: oxygen, powered medical equipment, insulin/refrigerated medicine, mobility aids, pregnancy, infants, older adults, cognitive/disability support. Assign roles: safety, medical, water/sanitation, communications, supplies/log.

============================================================
02 SEVERE BLEEDING, BREATHING, CPR, SHOCK
============================================================

SEVERE BLEEDING
Expose the wound only as needed. Apply firm continuous direct pressure with gauze or the cleanest available cloth. Do not keep lifting the dressing to inspect. If it soaks through, add material and maintain pressure.

For a deep wound suitable for packing, push gauze/clean cloth firmly into the wound cavity and continue pressure. Do not pack open chest or abdominal wounds or pack around a deeply embedded object.

For life-threatening arm/leg bleeding not controlled quickly by direct pressure, use a commercial tourniquet if available: place above the wound, not across a joint, tighten until bleeding stops, record time, and do not periodically loosen it. Improvised tourniquets fail more often and can injure; direct pressure/packing is preferable when effective.

Do not remove deeply embedded objects. Stabilize them and control bleeding around them.

UNRESPONSIVE / NOT BREATHING NORMALLY
Call/get help and an AED if any are available. For a suddenly collapsed adult/teen who is unresponsive and not breathing normally, place on a firm surface and give hard, fast chest compressions in the center of the chest, about 100-120/min, allowing recoil. Follow AED prompts as soon as available. Continue until normal breathing, trained help takes over, the scene becomes unsafe, or you cannot continue. If trained in full CPR/rescue breathing, use that training. Children, infants, drowning, overdose, and prolonged respiratory arrest benefit particularly from ventilation; use trained techniques when known.

CHOKING
If a person can cough/speak/breathe, encourage coughing. If they cannot, summon help and use standard choking first aid you have been trained to perform. Technique differs for infants and during pregnancy/large body habitus. If unresponsive, start CPR; remove only an object you can actually see, never a blind finger sweep.

SHOCK
After trauma, shock may show as weakness, pale/cool/clammy skin, rapid breathing, confusion, faintness, thirst, or deterioration. Stop bleeding, keep the person lying in a tolerable position, protect from temperature extremes, reassure, and evacuate. Do not give food/drink to a severely injured, vomiting, or poorly conscious person or someone likely to need surgery.

============================================================
03 TRAUMA, BURNS, WOUNDS, FRACTURES, EYES
============================================================

HEAD/NECK/SPINE
After major fall/crash/diving injury/head blow, minimize unnecessary movement when there is neck/back pain, weakness/numbness, confusion, repeated vomiting, worsening headache, seizure, unequal pupils, or loss of consciousness. Airway, breathing, bleeding, and immediate hazards take priority over perfect immobilization. Worsening neurologic signs need urgent evacuation.

BURNS
Stop the burning process without endangering yourself. For ordinary thermal burns, cool with clean cool running water when available; avoid ice directly on significant burns and folk remedies such as butter/grease/toothpaste. Remove rings/watches/tight objects before swelling if not stuck. Cover loosely with clean non-stick material. Large/deep burns; electrical/chemical/inhalation burns; burns of face/hands/genitals/major joints; circumferential burns; or breathing problems need urgent professional care.

Chemical skin/eye exposure: protect yourself, remove contaminated clothing, brush off dry chemicals before irrigation unless product-specific instructions say otherwise, and irrigate liquid contamination with lots of clean water. Do not attempt acid/base neutralization on the body. Eye chemical exposure requires immediate prolonged irrigation while help is sought.

Electrical injury can cause hidden internal damage and dangerous heart rhythms even with small skin burns. Ensure power is disconnected before touching a casualty.

WOUNDS
Wash hands. Rinse dirty wounds generously with safe water. Gently remove loose superficial debris that is easily accessible; do not dig for deep foreign objects. Mild soap around a wound is reasonable. Do not repeatedly pour harsh antiseptics into tissue. Cover with a clean dressing; change when wet/dirty. Bites, deep punctures, crushing wounds, gross contamination, retained objects, loss of function, or spreading redness/swelling/pus/fever/red streaks need medical care when reachable. Tetanus status matters.

FRACTURES
Treat severe deformity, exposed bone, major swelling after trauma, inability to use a limb, or severe focal pain as possible fracture. Control bleeding. Do not repeatedly manipulate or force straight. Pad and splint in the position found when practical, supporting above and below injury. Check warmth/color/sensation/circulation before and after splinting; loosen if the splint compromises circulation. Open fractures need clean coverage and urgent care.

SPRAINS
Protect/rest. Compression can reduce swelling if comfortable and not impairing circulation. Elevate. Use wrapped cold packs for short periods if available. Severe pain, deformity, inability to bear weight, numbness, or persistent dysfunction may represent fracture/serious injury.

EYE
Loose irritant: flush with clean water. Chemical: irrigate immediately and continuously. Embedded/penetrating object: do not remove or press; shield without pressure and evacuate. Sudden vision loss, severe eye pain, penetrating injury, or chemical burn is urgent.

04 ILLNESS, DEHYDRATION, MEDICINES
============================================================

DEHYDRATION
Frequent small sips may be tolerated better than large volumes. Danger signs include very little urine, inability to keep fluid down, fainting/marked dizziness, confusion, extreme lethargy, rapid deterioration, or dehydration in infants/older adults.

EMERGENCY ORS
If commercial oral rehydration solution is unavailable: 1 liter safe water + 6 level teaspoons sugar + 1/2 level teaspoon salt. Measure carefully; excess salt is dangerous. Give frequent small amounts, especially after diarrhea/vomiting. Continue breastfeeding. Bloody diarrhea, severe abdominal pain, persistent high fever, shock, or severe dehydration needs professional care.

RESPIRATORY ILLNESS
Rest, fluids, ventilation when weather permits, hand hygiene, cough etiquette, and separation from especially vulnerable people reduce harm. Well-fitting masks can reduce respiratory spread when appropriate. Difficulty breathing, blue/gray lips, severe chest pain, new confusion, seizure, inability to stay awake, or rapid worsening is urgent.

FEVER
Treat the person, not the number alone: fluids, rest, comfortable temperature. OTC fever/pain medicines should be used only according to labels and individual contraindications. Fever with stiff neck, severe headache/confusion, severe breathing trouble, widespread concerning rash, seizure, or shock needs urgent assessment.

MEDICINE INVENTORY
For each person record: drug; strength; usual dose/schedule; reason; allergies; prescriber/pharmacy; critical storage needs. Keep original labels when possible. Do not casually share prescriptions or double missed doses unless instructions explicitly say so. Preserve refrigerated medicines using correct cool storage without freezing; exact stability is product-specific.

OTC SAFETY
Read active ingredients. Combination cold/flu products often duplicate acetaminophen or other ingredients. NSAIDs (ibuprofen/naproxen) can be unsuitable with kidney disease, ulcers, bleeding/anticoagulants, severe dehydration, and some pregnancy stages. Aspirin has special bleeding risks and is generally avoided in children/teens with viral illness. Follow labeled dosing rather than improvising.

ANTIBIOTICS
Do not use leftover/random antibiotics as a general disaster cure. Drug choice, allergy, dose, duration, kidney function, pregnancy, organism, and source control matter. A wound that needs drainage or a tooth that needs dental treatment may not be fixed by pills. Preserve labeled courses for the person/condition prescribed and seek clinical guidance when possible.

05 WATER: SOURCE, TREATMENT, STORAGE
============================================================

Stored known-safe water is best. A common planning target is about 1 US gallon/4 L per person/day for drinking and basic sanitation; heat, illness, pregnancy/lactation, heavy work, and pets increase need.

POSSIBLE HOUSEHOLD SOURCES
Commercial bottled water; clean stored containers; melted clean ice; food liquids; a conventional tank water heater if not chemically contaminated and safely isolated; water trapped in potable pipes. Toilet tank water is only a possible source if no chemical cleaner/contamination exists. Never use toilet-bowl water for drinking.

DO NOT DRINK water from hydronic heating boilers/radiators, waterbeds, chemically treated pools/spas, floodwater, or water smelling/looking contaminated by fuel, solvent, firefighting chemicals, industrial runoff, saltwater, or unknown chemicals. Boiling kills organisms but does not remove salt, fuels, many metals, pesticides, or other dissolved chemicals and may concentrate them.

BIOLOGICAL TREATMENT
If cloudy, let settle and prefilter through clean cloth/coffee filter. Bring clear water to a rolling boil at least 1 minute; above about 6,500 ft/2,000 m use 3 minutes. Cool covered in a clean container.

Filters are specific: many camping microfilters remove bacteria/protozoa but not viruses; activated carbon may reduce some tastes/chemicals but is not universal; particulate filters do not make chemically contaminated water safe. Read actual performance claims.

Chemical disinfectants depend on product concentration, water temperature/turbidity, contact time, and organism. Household bleach concentrations vary substantially. Only use plain unscented disinfecting bleach and follow emergency-water directions for that exact concentration from the label/authoritative instructions; do not preserve one old drops-per-gallon recipe as universal.

SEAWATER
Do not drink seawater. Ordinary boiling without capturing condensed steam does not remove salt. Distillation can produce fresh water if vapor is condensed into a separate clean container, but it consumes fuel and some volatile chemicals can carry over; source quality still matters.

RAIN
Rain itself may be relatively clean but roofs/gutters can add bird feces, ash, lead, chemicals, or debris. First-flush diversion and treatment reduce microbial risk. After wildfire/industrial release, contamination may be chemical rather than only biological.

FLOODWATER
Assume contaminated. Do not use for drinking, tooth brushing, food preparation, dishwashing, or wound care unless properly treated and no chemical contamination is suspected. Flooding can also contaminate private wells; disinfection/testing after flood matters.

STORAGE
Use food-grade containers never used for toxic chemicals. Keep closed, cool/dark when possible, away from fuel/pesticide vapors. Label SAFE DRINKING / UNTREATED / CLEANING. Pour rather than dipping hands/cups. Protect openings.

CONSERVATION
Do not dangerously ration drinking water while wasting water elsewhere. Cut laundry/bathing, use one-pot cooking, reuse noncontaminated gray water only for suitable non-drinking tasks, and repair leaks. Never mix gray water into drinking storage.

============================================================
06 SANITATION, TOILETS, HANDS, WASTE
============================================================

Once sewer/water systems fail, sanitation becomes a primary survival system.

HANDS
Wash with soap and safe water after toileting/diapers/waste, before food/eating, before/after wound care, and after raw animal products. Friction and rinsing matter. Alcohol hand sanitizer >=60% is useful when hands are not visibly dirty but is not a universal substitute for soap/water, particularly for some diarrheal pathogens or chemicals.

SEWER FAILURE
Stop flushing when authorities warn sewer lines are unavailable or when backup occurs. A toilet connected to a failed sewer can bring contamination into the home.

BUCKET/DRY TOILET
Use a sturdy lined bucket or lined disabled toilet. Add dry absorbent material to reduce liquid/odor. Close bags/containers; isolate from children, animals, food/water, and living/food-prep areas. Wash hands after handling. Separating urine can reduce volume but neither urine nor feces should be treated as automatically harmless.

DISPOSAL
Local soil, groundwater, flood risk, density, climate, and rules determine safe burial/containment. Keep human waste far from wells, surface water, food gardens, flood zones, and high groundwater. Prefer controlled collection when it returns. Do not create a neighborhood groundwater problem to solve a household toilet problem.

DIAPERS/MENSTRUAL/CONTINENCE
Plan separate sealed waste, handwashing, skin cleaning, barrier products as needed, and extra water/supplies. Skin breakdown becomes a medical problem during prolonged outages.

GARBAGE
Separate human waste, food waste, hazardous chemicals/batteries, sharps, and ordinary dry trash. Secure against insects/rodents. Do not burn plastics, batteries, electronics, treated/painted wood, or unknown chemicals; toxic smoke may cause severe harm.

DEAD ANIMALS
Avoid bare-hand contact. Keep carcasses away from water/food and follow public-health disposal instructions when available. Sudden clusters of unexplained animal deaths may indicate environmental/biological hazards.

============================================================
07 FOOD SAFETY, POWER FAILURE, COOKING
============================================================

POWER OUT
Keep refrigerator/freezer doors closed. Use a thermometer if available. Perishable food is generally safest at <=40 F/4 C. Time above safe temperature, ambient heat, food type, and initial temperature matter. After uncertain prolonged warming, discard high-risk perishables; smell/taste cannot reliably detect pathogens/toxins.

High-risk: meat, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy, cooked leftovers, cooked rice/pasta, many prepared foods, cut melons, sprouts. Shelf-stable dry/canned foods are less dependent on refrigeration if packaging remains intact.

FREEZER
A full, unopened freezer retains cold much longer than a refrigerator and generally longer than a half-full freezer. Ice crystals/frozen state and thermometer readings are better evidence than guesswork. Refreezing quality may suffer even when safety is acceptable.

CANS/JARS
Discard containers that are bulging, leaking, badly damaged at seams, spurting/foaming unexpectedly, or clearly compromised. Never taste suspicious canned food. Home-canned low-acid foods can carry botulism risk when not processed by validated pressure-canning methods.

COOKING
Outdoor combustion is the safe default. Never use charcoal grills, generators, gasoline engines, or outdoor-rated combustion appliances in homes/garages. Camp/propane/butane stoves must follow their manufacturer indoor/outdoor rating; an open window does not make an outdoor-only appliance safe. Keep CO alarms working.

FUEL
Let hot appliances cool before refueling. Store fuel in proper containers away from occupied areas/ignition. Never use gasoline as a fire accelerant. Do not bring propane cylinders into living spaces beyond what applicable appliance/storage rules allow.

PANTRY
Rotate foods you actually eat. Useful resilient foods: grains/rice/oats/pasta; beans/lentils; canned meat/fish/beans/vegetables/fruit; nut/seed butters; shelf-stable milk; oil/fat; salt; sugar; spices; nutrition appropriate to allergies/medical needs. Protect from heat, moisture, pests. Fats generally become rancid sooner than sugar/salt/dry white rice.

NUTRITION
Short term: safe water and calories dominate. Weeks/months: adequate protein, essential fat, varied micronutrients, fiber, and appropriate foods for infants/pregnancy/illness become important. Do not rely indefinitely on rice/beans alone without broader nutrition.

08 FIRE, CO, SMOKE, GENERATORS
============================================================

CARBON MONOXIDE is invisible and odorless. Headache, nausea, dizziness, weakness, confusion, chest pain, collapse, or several people/pets becoming ill in the same space should raise suspicion. Move to fresh air immediately and seek emergency care; do not re-enter until source is controlled.

GENERATOR
Run outdoors, well away from windows, doors, vents, crawlspaces, and attached garages; point exhaust away. Never in a basement/garage/enclosed porch or improvised enclosure. Protect from weather without trapping exhaust. Refuel only after cooling.

Never backfeed a house through a receptacle or improvised male-to-male cord. Safe building connection requires approved transfer equipment/interlock and correct installation; backfeed can kill utility workers and occupants.

FIRE ESCAPE
Smoke alarms save time. During fire, leave, close doors behind if possible, crawl low only when smoke conditions require it, avoid elevators unless responders direct otherwise, meet outside, and stay out. A fire extinguisher is for a small contained fire when you know how to use it and have an unobstructed escape behind you. If it does not control quickly, leave.

CANDLES
Prefer LEDs. If unavoidable, keep candles stable, attended, away from curtains/bedding/paper/fuel/oxygen, children, and pets.

WILDFIRE SMOKE
Stay in cleaner indoor air if evacuation/fire risk does not require leaving. Close infiltration where practical, use filtration if power permits. N95/P100 respirators reduce particles when well fitted but do not remove CO or most toxic gases and do not supply oxygen.

============================================================
09 COLD, HEAT, SHELTER
============================================================

COLD
Stay dry. Block wind/rain. Layer dry clothing: base, insulation, wind/water barrier. Protect head/hands/feet without impairing circulation. Insulate underneath sleeping people with pads, cardboard, blankets, dry vegetation inside a barrier, etc. A smaller occupied safe room is easier to warm than an entire building.

HYPOTHERMIA
Shivering, clumsiness, confusion, slurred speech, unusual behavior, drowsiness; severe cases may stop shivering. Handle gently, remove wet clothing, insulate, warm the torso gradually, and seek help. Give warm sweet fluids only if fully alert and able to swallow. Alcohol worsens heat loss/judgment.

FROSTBITE
Protect from further cold/refreezing; do not rub with snow. Refreezing after thaw can cause major damage, so do not thaw deeply frostbitten tissue if unavoidable refreezing is likely. Seek medical care.

HEAT
Shade, airflow, reduce exertion, drink fluids, cool skin. Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, headache, nausea. Heat stroke: altered mental status, collapse, seizure, severe overheating - medical emergency. Begin rapid cooling immediately: move to shade/cooler area, remove excess clothing, wet skin and fan; cold-water immersion is highly effective when feasible/safe, with continuous monitoring and emergency transport. Do not delay cooling waiting for help.

SHELTER SELECTION
Avoid flood channels, unstable trees/walls, avalanche paths, landslide slopes, exposed ridges during lightning, and structures with major fire/structural damage. Ventilation is necessary for any permitted combustion appliance; no ventilation scheme makes a generator or charcoal grill acceptable indoors.

============================================================
10 ELECTRICITY, BATTERIES, SOLAR
============================================================

Assume mains conductors are dangerous unless properly de-energized/tested. Water and damaged wiring multiply risk. Do not enter water to access panels or appliances.

LOADS
For simple DC/resistive loads: watts = volts x amps. watt-hours = watts x hours. A 100 W load for 5 h needs 500 Wh before conversion losses. Inverters, batteries, wiring, and fuses must be sized for current and surge; high-power heating/cooking depletes batteries quickly.

12 V BATTERIES
Lead-acid/automotive batteries can deliver enormous short-circuit current. Protect positive wiring with appropriately sized fuses near source, prevent tools/jewelry bridging terminals, observe polarity, ventilate during charging, and avoid sparks/flames. Starting batteries are not ideal for repeated deep cycling.

LITHIUM
Stop using cells/packs that swell, hiss, vent, become abnormally hot, smoke, or show severe damage. Do not puncture/crush. Isolate from combustibles if safe. Lithium battery fires can reignite; get fire-service help when possible.

SOLAR
Panel -> appropriate charge controller -> compatible battery is the usual small off-grid chain; then DC loads or an appropriately rated inverter. Do not connect arbitrary panels directly to bare lithium cells. Shading one portion can sharply reduce output depending on configuration. Aim panels toward unobstructed sun and keep surfaces reasonably clean.

EXTENSION CORDS
Use intact cords rated for load/environment; keep connections dry, avoid rugs/pinch points, uncoil under high load, do not defeat ground pins. Space heaters/hot plates/kettles are high-load devices.

11 COMMUNICATIONS AND INFORMATION INTEGRITY
============================================================

PAPER FIRST
Write critical contacts, addresses, medication list, meeting points, and one out-of-area contact. Print/map routes before networks fail.

MESSAGE FORMAT
TIME / FROM / LOCATION / STATUS / NEED / NEXT ACTION.
Example: 1540 / LEE FAMILY / SCHOOL GYM / 4 SAFE / NEED INSULIN / NEXT CHECK 1800.
Short, factual messages reduce ambiguity and airtime.

CELL NETWORK
During congestion, brief SMS may succeed when voice calls fail. Avoid repeated calls that waste battery/network. Send one status message to a relay/out-of-area contact. Turn off unnecessary wireless services and reduce screen brightness; periodically check networks rather than searching continuously when no service exists.

RADIO
Listen first. Identify as required. Plain language. Repeat important numbers slowly. Log source/time. FRS is short-range and simple; terrain/buildings greatly reduce range. GMRS/amateur radio rules vary and repeaters may fail without backup power. A better/higher antenna location often matters more than nominal transmitter power.

RUMOR CONTROL
Record what was directly observed versus reported. Preserve source/time. Confirm high-consequence claims before acting when delay is safe. Do not turn "someone smelled chemicals" into "the city is poisoned." Corrections should travel on the same channels as the original rumor.

PHYSICAL NETWORK
Use community boards, runners, scheduled check points, and written receipts. A runner should carry copies, not the sole record. Standard message forms prevent reinterpretation.

============================================================
12 NAVIGATION, EVACUATION, LOST PERSON
============================================================

EVACUATE EARLY for official orders or clearly worsening fire/flood/structural/chemical threats. Take people, critical meds, IDs/documents, water, clothing, mobility aids, communications, pets when possible. Do not delay for possessions.

Have primary and alternate routes plus a walking fallback. Avoid hazard-specific chokepoints: low roads in flood, canyons/one-way roads in wildfire, damaged bridges after quake, coast after tsunami warning, unstable slopes after intense rain.

MEETING POINTS
Choose near-home, outside-neighborhood, and out-of-region points. If leaving a note, include date/time/destination without unnecessarily revealing valuables/security details.

MAP
Keep paper map. Mark north, home, water, hospitals/clinics, shelters, bridges, industrial hazards, high ground, alternate routes. Compass + known landmark beats improvised folklore.

LOST
Stop; prevent panic-driven wandering. Determine last known location, daylight, weather, water, injuries, likely search behavior. If rescuers know your route, staying near it can improve discovery unless location is unsafe. Signal with whistle/light/contrast/radio. Conserve phone battery but attempt emergency call/text where any network may exist.

============================================================
13 EARTHQUAKE, FLOOD, WIND, TSUNAMI, WILDFIRE
============================================================

EARTHQUAKE
During strong shaking: Drop, Cover, Hold On; protect head/neck, stay away from glass. Do not run through falling exterior debris. After: expect aftershocks, wear sturdy shoes/gloves, check fire/gas/electrical hazards, leave seriously damaged structures, avoid elevators, conserve phones.

FLOOD
Move to high ground before routes close. Never enter moving floodwater. Leave a basement if rapid flooding is possible. After flooding, assume water, mud, food, wells, electrical systems, and soaked porous materials may be contaminated.

HURRICANE/HIGH WIND
Evacuate storm-surge zones when directed; an upper floor is not a reliable substitute for surge evacuation. During wind in a safe nonflooding building, use a small interior room away from windows. The calm eye is temporary.

TORNADO
Basement/storm shelter or small interior room on lowest safe floor, away from windows. Protect head/neck with arms/helmet/mattress/sturdy covering. Manufactured homes are highly vulnerable; reach a designated sturdy shelter if time.

TSUNAMI
After a strong/long coastal earthquake, sudden sea withdrawal/rise, natural warning, or official alert, immediately go inland/uphill. Do not wait to photograph. Multiple waves can arrive for hours; first may not be largest.

WILDFIRE
Leave early. Take meds/documents/water, close building openings if time, and choose routes away from fire/smoke. Do not assume ordinary respirators or garden hoses make an advancing fire survivable.

LANDSLIDE/DEBRIS FLOW
Move out of channel/path toward stable higher ground. Burn scars can generate debris flows in heavy rain. Do not cross an active flow.

============================================================
14 CHEMICAL, RADIOLOGICAL, BIOLOGICAL EVENTS
============================================================

CHEMICAL RELEASE
Distance and correct shelter/evacuation matter. If told to shelter: enter a substantial building, close doors/windows, shut ventilation that draws outdoor air if safe, move to an interior area, monitor instructions. If contaminated: protect helpers, remove outer clothing, bag/isolate it, rinse exposed skin with water when appropriate. A wet cloth or N95 is not protection against most toxic gases/vapors.

FALLOUT
For external fallout radiation: get inside quickly; maximize distance from outside/roof and put dense material between you and outdoors. Basement or building center is usually better than windows/exterior. Remove outer clothing carefully and isolate it; shower with soap/water if available without harsh scrubbing. Stay sheltered and use official measurements/instructions when they become available. Potassium iodide only protects the thyroid from radioactive iodine, has risks, and should be taken only when health authorities recommend it for that event.

INFECTIOUS OUTBREAK
Clean air, ventilation/filtration, staying home when ill, avoiding crowded close contact during high transmission, well-fitting masks when indicated, hand hygiene, and protecting high-risk people are general tools. Pathogen-specific routes differ; current public-health guidance outranks generic rules.

============================================================
15 GAS, WATER, HOUSE SYSTEMS, REPAIRS
============================================================

Before emergencies learn locations of water main, electrical panel, gas control, sewer cleanout if applicable. Label tools.

GAS
Do not shut gas off casually if restoration procedure is unknown; many systems require utility/qualified restart. For odor/hissing/obvious damage: leave, avoid sparks, contact utility/fire service. Never search for leaks with an open flame.

BURST PIPE
If accessible without electrical/structural danger, close local/main water valve. Protect electrical equipment only if safe. Drain/relieve pressure as appropriate. Temporary clamps/rubber patches can reduce leakage but require monitoring and are not permanent repairs.

ROOF
Interior water damage is preferable to a fall. Avoid roof work in wind/rain/ice/darkness, on damaged structures, or without fall-safety competence. Catch water, move possessions, protect electrical areas, and tarp externally only when conditions make it safely possible.

STRUCTURE
New large cracks, leaning walls, sagging floors/roof, foundation movement, unusual popping, doors suddenly jammed throughout a structure, or fire/flood impact may indicate instability. Leave if collapse is plausible; do not diagnose a major structure from a short guide.

TOOLS
Useful general kit: adjustable wrenches, pliers, screwdrivers, hammer, hand saw/hacksaw, utility knife, tape measure, fasteners, duct/repair tape, rope/cord, buckets, shovel, pry bar, tarps/plastic, work gloves, eye protection, headlamp, extinguisher. Multimeters and electrical tools help only when users understand safe measurement.

============================================================
16 COMMUNITY OPERATIONS
============================================================

Organize skills, not just supplies. Identify medical/nursing, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, construction, radio, languages, childcare, elder/disability care, food/logistics, conflict mediation.

DAILY BRIEF
1 people/injuries;
2 hazards/weather;
3 water;
4 sanitation;
5 medicines/health;
6 food/fuel/power;
7 communications;
8 work assignments/buddies;
9 next check-in.
Keep a written log.

ZONES
Separate clean food/water, sick care, toilet/waste, fuel/chemicals, repair/dust, sleeping. Mark unsafe rooms/structures.

SECURITY
Prioritize locks, lighting, controlled access, knowing neighbors, buddy movement, discretion about supplies, de-escalation, and clear rules. Improvised traps/electrified barriers/uncontrolled fire can injure allies and responders. Prevent conflict before it consumes scarce resources.

RATIONING
Inventory before rationing. Define units and measurement. Publish rules. Reserve life-critical medical water/power/food first, then protect shared systems and vulnerable people. Recalculate as supply and population change. Secret arbitrary rationing destroys trust.

============================================================
17 CHILDREN, INFANTS, OLDER ADULTS, DISABILITY, PREGNANCY
============================================================

Children lose heat and water faster relative to size and may not describe symptoms. Preserve routine/supervision and keep hazards/medicines/fuel out of reach.

INFANTS
Breastfeeding, if established/possible, reduces dependence on water/sterilization. Formula must be mixed exactly as manufacturer directs with safe water and clean equipment; never stretch by adding extra water. Maintain warmth without overheating and monitor wet diapers/feeding as hydration clues.

OLDER ADULTS
Watch medication continuity, dehydration, temperature vulnerability, falls, hearing/vision, cognition, and mobility. Do not assume confusion is "just age"; infection, dehydration, hypoxia, medication problems, heat/cold, stroke, or head injury can cause acute change.

DISABILITY
Keep mobility aids, glasses, hearing-aid batteries, chargers, communication boards/devices, continence/feeding supplies, service-animal needs, and backup assistance accessible. Evacuation routes must be physically usable by the person.

PREGNANCY
Hydration, nutrition, infection prevention, and heat avoidance matter. Heavy bleeding, seizure, severe headache/vision change, severe abdominal pain, difficulty breathing, labor concerns, or significant reduction in established fetal movement warrants urgent obstetric care. Do not delay available skilled care to attempt improvised childbirth procedures from a field note.

============================================================
18 LONGER-TERM FOOD AND AGRICULTURE
============================================================

A garden solves future food, not today's calories. Stabilize water/sanitation/shelter first.

CROP CHOICE
Use local climate/season. Favor a mix: calorie crops (potato/sweet potato/corn where suitable), legumes, quick greens, roots, squash/storage crops, herbs. Diversity reduces single-crop failure.

SOIL
Avoid food production in soil contaminated by sewage, industrial/fuel chemicals, flood sediment of unknown source, lead-painted building debris, or ash from burned structures until assessed. Raised clean soil can help but contaminated irrigation/air remains a pathway.

WATERING
Mulch and early/late watering reduce evaporation. Drip/root watering saves water versus broad spraying. Do not use fecally contaminated water on edible crops, especially foods eaten raw.

COMPOST
Plant residues and suitable food scraps become soil amendment when managed for air/moisture/carbon/nitrogen. Keep hazardous chemicals, unknown ash, pet/human feces, treated wood, and contaminated material out of food-garden compost absent validated treatment.

SEEDS
Store cool/dry/dark, labeled crop/variety/year. Seed viability varies. Open-pollinated crops can reproduce more predictably than hybrids, but seed saving requires species-specific isolation and maturity knowledge.

PRESERVATION
Drying, fermentation, freezing, water-bath canning, pressure canning are different processes. Low-acid foods require validated pressure-canning recipes; botulism toxin may have no smell/taste. Never invent canning times/pressures.

============================================================
19 MECHANICS, MATERIALS, SIMPLE ENGINEERING
============================================================

Do not stand beneath suspended loads. Do not use unverified ropes/anchors for lifting people. Unknown structures/materials deserve large safety margins.

LEVER
Torque = force x perpendicular distance from pivot. Longer handles reduce required force but increase load on tool/fastener and can cause sudden failure.

PULLEY
Ideal mechanical advantage roughly equals number of rope segments supporting a moving load, at cost of more rope travel; friction reduces real advantage. Anchors must carry resulting loads.

ROPES
Common functions: bowline=fixed loop; figure-eight=stopper/loop family; clove hitch=quick temporary attachment; two half hitches=secure line; trucker's hitch=tension. Knots reduce rope strength. Practice before relying on them.

FASTENERS
Load direction matters. Screws/nails in weak/rotted material may pull out. Spread load with washers/plates where useful. Use multiple independent attachments for critical temporary work. Tape is excellent for sealing/bundling, poor as sole structural support.

WOOD
Keep critical wood dry and ventilated. Splits around fasteners reduce capacity. Pre-drilling near ends can reduce splitting. Temporary bracing works best as triangles rather than flexible rectangles.

METAL
Sharp edges and hot work cause injuries/fire. Grinding/welding need eye/face protection and fire watch; never weld/cut unknown closed containers. Galvanized/painted/contaminated metals can emit hazardous fumes when heated.

============================================================
20 BASIC PLUMBING AND WATER SYSTEMS
============================================================

PRESSURE
Water moves from higher pressure/head to lower. About 2.31 ft of water height produces 1 psi (or about 0.433 psi/ft); 10 m water head is roughly 1 bar/100 kPa. Real flow depends strongly on pipe diameter, length, roughness, valves, and elevation.

GRAVITY STORAGE
Elevated water creates pressure but is extremely heavy. 1 L water ~1 kg; 1 US gallon ~8.34 lb. A 55-gal drum contains about 459 lb/208 kg of water before container/support. Never put large tanks on floors/roofs/platforms not designed for the load.

SIPHON
A filled hose can move liquid from a higher source surface to a lower outlet if the hose remains filled and the outlet stays lower; do not mouth-siphon fuels/chemicals. A siphon cannot lift water over arbitrarily high barriers and cannot create energy.

BACKFLOW
Do not connect unsafe water to potable plumbing without proper backflow protection; pressure changes can contaminate the clean system.

============================================================
21 ELECTRICAL REFERENCE FOR SAFE LOW-VOLTAGE WORK
============================================================

V = I*R (Ohm's law) for appropriate simple circuits.
P = V*I.
Energy Wh = power W * time h.
Series resistances add. Parallel resistors: 1/Rt=1/R1+1/R2+...

Example: 12 V load drawing 5 A uses about 60 W. For 4 h it needs ~240 Wh plus losses. A nominal 12 V 100 Ah battery contains ~1200 Wh at rated conditions, but usable energy depends on battery chemistry, discharge rate, temperature, age, and desired life.

WIRE
Excess current through undersized wire causes heat/fire. Fuse/breaker protects wiring, not merely the appliance. Put source-side protection close to batteries. Long low-voltage runs lose voltage; larger conductors reduce resistance.

AC MAINS
Do not infer safe mains practice from these formulas. Mains can kill; damaged grounding, neutral faults, wet conditions, generators, and multiple sources complicate isolation. Use qualified help when available.

============================================================
22 COMMUNICATION NETWORK WITHOUT INTERNET
============================================================

Build a layered system:
LEVEL 1 voice/whistle in building;
LEVEL 2 written door/message board;
LEVEL 3 runners/bicycles;
LEVEL 4 FRS/GMRS/local radio as lawful/equipped;
LEVEL 5 cellular SMS;
LEVEL 6 amateur/HF/satellite where equipped.
No layer should be the sole plan.

CHECK-IN SCHEDULE
Pick fixed windows e.g. top of every even hour rather than leaving receivers/transmitters active continuously. Record missed check-ins before dispatching searchers; people may be conserving power.

MESSAGE NUMBERING
Station/household assigns sequential message numbers. Log SEND time, recipient, acknowledgement. If relayed, preserve original author/time and add relay time rather than rewriting content.

PRIORITY
1 immediate life threat;
2 missing/injured;
3 evacuation/hazard;
4 water/medical/logistics;
5 welfare/status;
6 routine.
Avoid clogging scarce channels with duplicate chatter.

============================================================
23 NAVIGATION AND MAP REASONING
============================================================

A map is a model, not reality; roads/bridges change after disaster.

COMPASS
Magnetic compass points toward magnetic north, not necessarily map/grid true north. Local declination matters for precise long-distance bearings; for neighborhood movement, landmark/map orientation may be sufficient. Keep compass away from steel/phones/speakers while reading.

DISTANCE
Measure map distance using scale. Walking pace varies strongly by terrain/load/injury. Estimate conservatively and preserve daylight.

DEAD RECKONING
Record starting point, bearing/direction, distance/time, landmarks, and turns. Frequent written fixes prevent cumulative memory error.

URBAN
Street signs may be gone. Building numbers, intersections, rail lines, waterways, terrain, towers, and sun position provide cross-checks. Do not use railroad tracks/tunnels/utility corridors as casual routes; trains/electricity/structural hazards may remain.

============================================================
24 WEATHER WITHOUT INTERNET
============================================================

Observe trend, not folklore. Rapid pressure fall often accompanies worsening storm systems if you have a barometer. Darkening clouds, gust fronts, thunder, rising water, wind shift, and official radio alerts matter more than sayings about sky color.

LIGHTNING
If thunder is audible, seek substantial building or hard-topped vehicle. Avoid isolated trees, open ridges/fields, open water, conductive fences, and plumbing/wired equipment when strikes are close. A tent is not lightning protection.

FLASH FLOOD
Heavy rain far upstream can flood a dry channel. Do not camp/shelter in washes, culverts, underpasses, or drainage channels when storms threaten.

WINTER STORM
Avoid unnecessary travel. If stranded with vehicle in safe location, staying with vehicle may improve visibility/shelter unless location itself is dangerous. Clear snow from exhaust area before periodically running engine; CO can enter a snow-blocked vehicle. Never run a vehicle in an enclosed garage.

============================================================
25 HYGIENE, DENTAL, SKIN, PESTS
============================================================

Small hygiene failures become disabling over weeks.

FEET
Keep dry; change socks; air footwear; treat hot spots/blisters before they enlarge. Do not deliberately rupture intact blisters unless function requires and you can maintain cleanliness. Redness spreading, pus, severe pain, fever, or red streaking suggests infection.

DENTAL
Brush with safe water; floss if available. Severe tooth pain, facial swelling, fever, difficulty swallowing/breathing, or swelling spreading into neck/eye is urgent. Do not place aspirin/caustic substances directly on gums.

SKIN
Keep folds dry, avoid prolonged wet clothing, clean minor cuts, control insects. Shared towels/razors can spread infection.

RODENTS
Secure food/waste. Ventilate closed rodent-infested spaces before cleaning. Avoid sweeping/vacuuming dry droppings in ways that aerosolize dust; wet/disinfect according to public-health guidance when available and use gloves/respiratory protection appropriate to risk.

============================================================
26 CLEANING AND DISINFECTION
============================================================

CLEANING removes dirt/organic material; DISINFECTION reduces microbes; they are not identical. Clean first when surface is visibly dirty.

Never mix bleach with ammonia, acids/vinegar, toilet-bowl cleaners, or unknown chemicals; toxic gases can form. Follow product label dilution/contact time because concentrations differ. Ventilate as appropriate and protect eyes/skin.

Use drinking-quality water for food-contact surfaces and infant/medical uses when feasible. In a severe shortage, prioritize hands, food equipment, wound care, and high-touch sick-care areas rather than cosmetic cleaning.

MOLD
After flooding, dry wet buildings rapidly if safe. Porous materials heavily contaminated by sewage or extensive mold may need removal. N95/P100 reduces spores/dust but not gases. Do not enter structurally unsafe or heavily contaminated buildings merely to salvage items.

============================================================
27 DOCUMENTS, MONEY, INVENTORY, LOGS
============================================================

Maintain paper copies of IDs, emergency contacts, prescriptions, insurance/policy numbers, critical account/contact information, property inventory, and local maps. Protect sensitive data from unnecessary exposure.

INCIDENT LOG
Date/time | source | observation | action | result | next check.
Medical log: person | symptom/injury | vitals if known | treatment | medicine/time | change.
Supply log: item | starting amount | used | remaining | rate/day | reorder/source.

Cash/barter outages: record every transaction/loan. Use consistent units and signatures/witnesses for significant community exchanges. Written records reduce later conflict.

Photograph property damage when safe, before cleanup when practical. Life safety outranks documentation.

SCAMS
After disasters, verify contractors, charities, payment requests, benefit applications, and officials through independent channels. Urgency + secrecy + irreversible payment/credentials is a warning combination.

============================================================
28 LOGISTICS AND RATIONING MATH
============================================================

DAYS OF SUPPLY = usable quantity / daily use.
Example: 72 L safe water / (4 people * 3 L drinking/cooking per person/day) = 6 days for that budget, excluding sanitation.

BURNDOWN
Record actual daily use rather than ideal estimate. If consumption exceeds plan, find leaks/waste and adjust early.

RESERVE
Do not hide uncertainty by treating every stored unit as usable. Deduct damaged/expired/questionable supply and keep contingency reserve for delays/illness.

CALORIES
Exact needs vary. In a short emergency, a rough adult planning figure often lands around 2,000 kcal/day, but body size, age, pregnancy/lactation, temperature, and heavy labor change it substantially. Do not impose adult ration math on infants/children or people with medical diets.

FUEL
Track by usable energy/task, not just container count: meals cooked, liters boiled, hours generator runtime at typical load. Preserve high-grade fuels for jobs that have no substitute.

============================================================
29 HAND TOOLS, CUTTING, LIFTING, WORK SAFETY
============================================================

Fatigue causes accidents. Stop when hands shake, light fades, footing worsens, or a job becomes improvised beyond skill.

EYE protection for hammering/cutting/grinding; gloves for sharp/dirty work but keep loose gloves away from rotating machinery; hearing protection around prolonged loud equipment; respirators matched to particles/vapors.

KNIFE
Cut away from body, stabilize work, sheath when moving. A dull blade often requires dangerous force.

LADDER
Stable level base, correct angle, maintain three points of contact, do not stand on top steps not intended for standing, avoid electrical lines, never improvise on boxes/chairs, have a spotter when useful.

JACK/LIFT
A jack lifts; rated stands/cribbing support. Never put body under a vehicle/load supported only by a jack. Chock wheels and use stable ground.

============================================================
30 VEHICLES AND FUEL
============================================================

Do not idle engines in garages or near occupied openings. CO risk persists even with garage door open.

FUEL
Gasoline vapor ignites easily. Store only approved quantities/containers where lawful, cool and away from ignition/living space. Fuel degrades; rotate. Never siphon by mouth.

STRANDED VEHICLE
If safe and rescuers can locate the road, vehicle offers shelter/visibility. Signal, conserve fuel, ventilate, and protect from heat/cold. In snow, keep tailpipe area clear before running engine periodically. If vehicle is in flood/fire/unstable location, move to safety instead.

TIRE
Change only on stable level ground away from traffic when possible: hazards/parking brake, chock opposite wheel, loosen lugs before lifting, jack at manufacturer point, never crawl underneath, tighten lug nuts in a cross/star sequence and recheck.

============================================================
31 LONGER-TERM WATER SYSTEMS
============================================================

SOURCE hierarchy: protected spring/well or treated municipal/store -> rain catchment -> surface water -> highly contaminated water, modified by local chemistry and upstream hazards.

WELL
Flooded wells can be microbially/chemically contaminated. Do not simply pump until clear and assume safe. Disinfection and testing are preferred when capability returns.

RAIN CATCHMENT
Roof -> first-flush/diversion -> screened inlet -> covered tank -> treatment for intended use. Keep insects/animals out. Tank overflow must drain away from foundations.

TANK SAFETY
Large tanks impose enormous structural load and can injure if supports fail. Secure against tipping, protect from contamination/light/algae, provide screened vent, and design drain/cleaning access.

============================================================
32 SHELTER IMPROVEMENT AND PASSIVE COMFORT
============================================================

HEAT RETENTION
Reduce air leakage that does not compromise combustion ventilation; occupy smaller zone; close unused rooms; use curtains/blankets with safe clearance from heaters; add insulation under/around sleepers. Sun-facing windows can admit solar heat by day, insulated coverings reduce loss at night.

COOLING
Shade exterior before heat enters, use cross-ventilation when outside air is cooler, exhaust hot air high, move people to lower/shaded levels, reduce indoor heat sources. Night flushing works where nights cool enough. High humidity reduces evaporative-cooling effectiveness.

MOISTURE
Ventilate cooking/bathing moisture when possible; condensation drives mold. Do not seal a building so tightly around combustion that oxygen/exhaust safety deteriorates.

============================================================
33 SIGNALS AND SEARCH
============================================================

Whistle carries farther than shouting for less energy. Three repeated signals are commonly recognized as distress, but any persistent patterned signal can attract attention. Contrast panels, mirrors/safe reflected light, flashlights, and ground markings help aircraft/searchers when visible.

If leaving a known route to seek help, leave a dated note with destination/direction and expected return; do not erase the only clue to your movement.

Search teams should use buddy system, boundaries, check-in times, and a written map/grid. Do not let an untrained search create additional missing people.

============================================================
34 BASIC MATH, UNITS, AND REFERENCE
============================================================

1 L=1000 mL. 1 kg=1000 g. 1 m=100 cm.
1 US gal=3.785 L. 1 qt=0.946 L. 1 lb=0.454 kg. 1 oz mass=28.35 g. 1 in=2.54 cm. 1 ft=0.3048 m. 1 mi=1.609 km.

C=(F-32)*5/9. F=C*9/5+32.
0 C=32 F; 20 C=68 F; 37 C=98.6 F; 100 C=212 F at sea level.

1 L fresh water ~=1 kg. 1 US gal water ~=8.34 lb.
Area rectangle=length*width. Triangle=base*height/2. Circle area=pi*r^2. Volume rectangular tank=L*W*H using consistent units.

Speed=distance/time. Density=mass/volume. Pressure=force/area. Power=energy/time.

Percent change=(new-old)/old*100%.
Slope grade %=rise/run*100.

============================================================
35 DECISION TREES
============================================================

PERSON DOWN?
Scene unsafe -> do not enter; summon/control hazard.
Safe -> severe bleeding? control it.
Unresponsive -> breathing normally? yes: recovery/monitor if no trauma prevents; no: CPR/AED.
Responsive -> severe breathing trouble/chest pain/stroke signs/major trauma? urgent evacuation.
Then protect temperature, document, reassess.

WATER NEEDED?
Known sealed/stored safe -> use.
Unknown biological source, no chemical suspicion -> settle/filter if dirty + boil/disinfect properly.
Fuel/solvent/salt/industrial/fallout chemical concern -> boiling not solution; find different source/special treatment.

NO POWER?
Medical device/refrigerated critical medicine -> highest energy priority/contact aid.
Then communications/light.
Then food preservation.
Heating/cooking by electricity is energy-intensive -> use safe alternatives without indoor CO/fire.

HOME UNSAFE?
Fire/gas/rising water/structural movement/official order -> leave.
No immediate threat -> identify one safe zone, shut/avoid damaged systems, monitor changes, plan alternate shelter.

============================================================
36 WHAT NOT TO IMPROVISE
============================================================

Do not improvise indoor generator exhaust.
Do not backfeed house wiring.
Do not mix cleaning chemicals.
Do not taste suspicious canned/spoiled food.
Do not drink seawater or chemically contaminated water.
Do not enter confined spaces (tanks, sewers, pits) because "it smells fine"; toxic/oxygen-deficient atmospheres may have no warning.
Do not perform live mains electrical repair from written instructions alone.
Do not weld/cut sealed unknown containers.
Do not walk a wet/damaged roof just to stop property damage.
Do not invent pressure-canning recipes.
Do not use unknown medications/antibiotics because services are down.
Do not use ordinary masks as protection from toxic gas/low oxygen.
Do not return to evacuated fire/flood/chemical zones merely because conditions look calmer.

============================================================
37 PRE-EVENT PREPARATION WITH HIGHEST RETURN
============================================================

Learn CPR/AED and bleeding control in person. Learn household water/gas/electrical controls. Maintain smoke/CO alarms and extinguishers. Practice evacuation/meeting points. Keep paper contacts/maps. Keep prescription buffer as legally/medically feasible. Rotate water/food. Back up documents offline. Maintain vehicles/tools/batteries. Know neighbors' skills.

BASIC CACHE
Water/containers/treatment; shelf-stable familiar food/manual opener; essential meds/list; first-aid + bleeding supplies; lights/headlamps; batteries/power banks; radio where useful; smoke/CO alarms; extinguisher; seasonal clothes/blankets; sanitation bags/buckets/soap; work gloves/eye protection/N95s; basic tools/tarps/cord/tape; maps/contacts/cash; chargers; infant/pet/disability-specific supplies.

============================================================
38 TRAINING PRIORITY
============================================================

If time before disaster is limited, practice in this order:
1 evacuation/fire/CO;
2 CPR/AED/bleeding;
3 water treatment and sanitation setup;
4 family communication/meeting points;
5 utility shutoff identification;
6 safe generator/fuel use if owned;
7 basic tool/repair safety;
8 radio/navigation;
9 food preservation/gardening for longer horizon.

A skill practiced once physically is often more retrievable under stress than three pages read during crisis.

============================================================
39 PRINCIPLES FOR RECOVERY BEYOND THE FIRST WEEKS
============================================================

Preserve PEOPLE before equipment; KNOWLEDGE before convenience; CLEAN WATER/SANITATION before expansion; MAINTENANCE before new construction; RECORDS before memory becomes disputed.

Standardize containers, units, labels, schedules, message formats, and repair parts where possible. Interchangeability reduces cognitive and supply burden.

Teach every critical task to at least two people. Rotate apprentices. Document local modifications and why they were made. A system understood by one expert is a single point of failure.

Cannibalize broken equipment methodically: label donor parts, preserve fasteners/manuals/connectors, avoid destroying a repairable machine for a low-value component unless priorities justify it.

Prioritize systems that multiply capability: clean water, sanitation, hand tools, lighting, charging, communications, transport, food storage, workshop space, records, education.

============================================================
40 FINAL RULES UNDER UNCERTAINTY
============================================================

1 Avoid the second casualty.
2 Fast fatal threats first: scene, bleeding, breathing, fire/CO, drowning, lethal temperature.
3 Safe water + sanitation outrank elaborate food acquisition.
4 Keep clean and dirty systems physically separate.
5 Smell/taste cannot prove safety for CO, microbes, chemicals, or spoiled food.
6 Invisible hazards deserve respect: electricity, CO, unstable structures, flood contamination, low oxygen.
7 Property damage is preferable to a risky roof, live-wire, confined-space, flood, or fire rescue.
8 Timestamp observations and distinguish direct evidence from rumor.
9 Redundancy beats a perfect single tool.
10 When a precise procedure is context-sensitive and expertise is unavailable, stabilize, isolate the hazard, preserve options, and seek qualified guidance rather than confidently improvising.
