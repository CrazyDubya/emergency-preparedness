EMERGENCY FIELD KNOWLEDGE - 100 KiB BUDGET

PURPOSE
For ordinary people facing prolonged failure of normal services. If reliable emergency services, evacuation orders, public-health guidance, utility instructions, poison control, or professional medical care exist, use them. This file emphasizes actions that are broadly safe under uncertainty. A precise instruction that is wrong for your situation can be worse than a general rule.

HOW TO USE
Stop immediate hazards first. Then: severe injury/breathing; safe water; fire/CO; temperature; sanitation; people/accountability; food; communications; power; recovery. Search plain text for: BLEEDING, CPR, WATER, DIARRHEA, FIRE, CO, COLD, HEAT, FOOD, TOILET, POWER, FLOOD, EARTHQUAKE, RADIO, EVACUATION, MEDICINE, REPAIR, GARDEN.

============================================================
01 IMMEDIATE PRIORITIES
============================================================
Do not become the next casualty. Look before touching, entering, climbing, wading, driving, switching, digging, or rescuing.
Leave fire, gas/chemical fumes, rapidly rising floodwater, obvious structural collapse, active violence, or an ordered evacuation. Property is replaceable. Close doors behind you during fire if safe. Once out of a burning building, stay out.
Treat downed electrical lines as live. Stay away from lines, wet ground around them, fences/rails touching them, and water that may be energized. Do not enter standing water to reach an electrical panel. If a line contacts a vehicle, occupants generally remain inside unless fire forces exit; avoid simultaneous contact with vehicle and ground.
Suspected gas leak: do not light flames, smoke, operate switches, start engines, or deliberately create sparks. Leave and contact the utility/fire service from a safe location if available.
Floodwater hides holes, debris, sewage, chemicals, and electricity. Do not drive, walk, or swim through moving floodwater merely because another vehicle crossed.
Count people. Identify children, older adults, pregnant people, people with disabilities, people dependent on refrigerated or powered medical equipment, and anyone missing. Assign simple roles: safety, first aid, water, communications, supplies.

============================================================
02 LIFE-THREATENING MEDICAL PROBLEMS
============================================================
GENERAL ORDER: safe scene; severe bleeding; airway/breathing; circulation/shock; dangerous heat/cold; professional help.
SEVERE BLEEDING: expose wound only as needed. Apply firm continuous pressure with gauze/clean cloth. Do not keep lifting the dressing. Add material on top if soaked. A deep compressible wound may be packed tightly with gauze/clean cloth and held under pressure. Do not pack open chest/abdomen or around embedded objects. For life-threatening arm/leg bleeding not controlled by pressure/packing, use a commercial tourniquet if available: above wound, not across a joint; tighten until bleeding stops; note time; do not periodically loosen. Do not remove deeply embedded objects.
UNRESPONSIVE/NOT BREATHING NORMALLY: call/get help and AED. For sudden adult/teen collapse, hard-fast chest compressions in center chest about 100-120/min, allow recoil. Follow AED prompts. If trained in full CPR/rescue breathing use it; children, drowning, overdose and respiratory arrest especially benefit from ventilation.
CHOKING: effective cough/speech -> encourage cough. Unable to breathe/speak/cough -> summon help and use trained choking first aid. Technique differs for infants and pregnancy/body shape. If unresponsive, CPR; remove only visible object, never blind-sweep.
SHOCK: weakness, pale/cool/clammy skin, rapid breathing, confusion, faintness, deterioration after injury. Control bleeding, lie in tolerable position, protect from temperature, reassure, evacuate. No food/drink for severely injured, vomiting, poorly conscious, likely surgical patient.

============================================================
03 TRAUMA, BURNS, WOUNDS, FRACTURES, EYES
============================================================
HEAD/NECK/SPINE: major fall/crash/diving injury/head blow plus neck/back pain, weakness/numbness, confusion, repeated vomiting, worsening headache, seizure, unequal pupils, loss of consciousness -> minimize unnecessary movement and seek urgent care. Airway/breathing/bleeding/immediate hazards outrank perfect immobilization.
BURNS: stop exposure safely. Thermal burn: cool with clean cool running water; avoid ice directly on significant burns and butter/grease/toothpaste. Remove rings/watches before swelling if not stuck. Cover loosely with clean non-stick material. Large/deep, electrical, chemical, inhalation, face/hands/genitals/joint/circumferential burns need urgent care. Chemical: protect yourself, remove contaminated clothing, brush off dry chemical before irrigation unless product instructions say otherwise, irrigate liquid exposure/eyes with lots of clean water. Do not neutralize acids/bases on body. Electrical injury may hide internal/heart damage; disconnect power first.
WOUNDS: wash hands; rinse generously with safe water; remove only loose accessible dirt; mild soap around wound; no harsh household chemicals in tissue. Clean dressing changed wet/dirty. Bites, deep punctures, crush/dirty wounds, retained material, loss of function, spreading redness/swelling/pus/fever/red streaks need care; tetanus matters.
FRACTURES: severe deformity/exposed bone/inability to use limb/major focal pain -> suspect fracture. Control bleeding. Do not force straight. Pad/splint in found position; support above/below; check color/warmth/sensation/circulation before/after. Open fracture: cover; do not push bone back.
EYE: loose dust -> irrigate. Chemical -> immediate copious irrigation. Embedded/penetrating -> do not remove/press; shield without pressure; urgent care.
POISON: protect rescuer; fresh air for inhalation if safe; remove contaminated clothes; do not automatically induce vomiting; keep label/container; use poison-control/emergency advice if available.

============================================================
04 ILLNESS, DEHYDRATION, MEDICINES
============================================================
DEHYDRATION: frequent small safe-fluid sips. Danger: very little urine, inability to keep fluids down, fainting/marked dizziness, confusion, lethargy, shock, infant/older adult severe dehydration.
ORS if commercial solution unavailable: 1 liter safe water + 6 level teaspoons sugar + 1/2 level teaspoon salt. Measure carefully; excess salt is dangerous. Give small frequent portions after diarrhea/vomiting. Continue breastfeeding. Bloody diarrhea, severe abdominal pain, persistent high fever, shock, severe dehydration -> professional care.
RESPIRATORY ILLNESS: rest, fluids, ventilation when weather permits, hand hygiene, cough etiquette, separation from vulnerable people, well-fitting masks when appropriate. Difficulty breathing, blue/gray lips, severe chest pain, new confusion, seizure, inability to stay awake, rapid worsening -> urgent.
FEVER: fluids/rest/comfort; OTC medicines only by label/contraindications. Fever plus stiff neck, severe confusion/headache, severe breathing trouble, seizure, shock -> urgent.
MEDICINES: record person/drug/strength/dose/schedule/reason/allergies/prescriber/storage. Keep original labels. Do not casually share prescriptions or double missed doses without instructions. Refrigerated drugs vary; cool without freezing and seek product-specific guidance.
OTC: read active ingredient. Combination cold/flu products duplicate drugs. NSAIDs may be unsuitable with kidney disease, ulcers, bleeding/anticoagulants, severe dehydration and some pregnancy stages. Aspirin has special risks and is generally avoided in children/teens with viral illness. Do not use leftover antibiotics as universal disaster medicine.

============================================================
05 WATER: SOURCE, TREATMENT, STORAGE
============================================================
Known-safe stored water best. Common planning target ~1 US gallon/4 L person/day drinking/basic sanitation; more in heat, illness, pregnancy/lactation, heavy work, pets.
POSSIBLE: sealed bottled/stored; clean ice; food liquids; conventional tank water heater if clean and safely isolated; potable plumbing. Toilet tank only if no cleaner/contamination; never bowl.
AVOID drinking hydronic boiler/radiator water, waterbeds, chemically treated pools/spas, floodwater, seawater, industrial runoff, fuel/solvent/pesticide/firefighting-foam contamination. Boiling does not remove salt/fuel/metals/most chemicals and can concentrate some.
BIOLOGICAL: cloudy -> settle/prefilter clean cloth. Rolling boil >=1 min; above ~6,500 ft/2,000 m use 3 min. Cool covered. Filters differ: many remove bacteria/protozoa, not viruses; particulate filtration not chemical purification; activated carbon is limited. Chemical disinfectants depend concentration/turbidity/temperature/contact time. Household bleach strength varies; only plain unscented disinfecting bleach with exact current instructions for that concentration, not one universal old recipe.
RAIN: roofs/gutters add feces/ash/lead/chemicals. First-flush, screened covered storage, treatment. Post-fire/industrial event may be chemically unsafe.
WELL: flood may contaminate; clear is not proof. Alternative safe source until inspected/disinfected/tested.
STORAGE: food-grade never-toxic containers; close/protect opening; label SAFE/UNTREATED/NONPOTABLE; away from fuels/pesticides. Pour rather than dip.
MASS: 1 L water ~1 kg; 1 gal ~8.34 lb. 55 gal ~459 lb water; do not overload floors/roofs.

============================================================
06 SANITATION, TOILETS, WASTE
============================================================
HANDS: soap+safe water after toilet/diaper/waste, before food/eating, before/after wound care, after raw animal product. Alcohol sanitizer >=60% useful when not visibly dirty but not universal for some diarrheal pathogens/chemicals.
SEWER FAILURE: if warning/backup, stop flushing. Use lined sturdy bucket/disabled toilet with dry absorbent material. Close/isolate waste from food/water/children/animals. Wash hands. Separating urine can reduce volume but neither urine nor feces should be assumed harmless.
DISPOSAL depends groundwater/soil/flood/density/rules. Keep human waste well away from wells, waterways, food gardens, flood zones, shallow groundwater. Prefer controlled collection when restored.
GARBAGE: separate human waste, food, sharps, batteries/electronics, chemicals, ordinary trash. Secure rodents/insects. Do not burn plastics, electronics, batteries, treated/painted wood, unknown chemicals.
DIAPERS/MENSTRUAL/CONTINENCE: sealed waste, handwashing, skin cleaning/barriers, extra supplies/water; prolonged skin breakdown becomes medical.

============================================================
07 FOOD SAFETY AND COOKING
============================================================
POWER OUT: keep fridge/freezer closed; thermometer if available. Perishables generally safest <=40 F/4 C. Time warm, ambient temperature, starting condition, food type matter. Uncertain prolonged warming -> discard high-risk perishables; smell/taste not proof. High-risk: meat/poultry/fish/eggs/dairy, cooked leftovers/rice/pasta, cut melon, sprouts, many prepared foods.
CANS/JARS: discard bulging/leaking/bad seam/rust-through or suspicious spurting/foaming. Never taste suspicious canned food. Low-acid home canning requires validated pressure process; botulism may have no smell/taste.
COOKING: outdoor combustion is default. Never charcoal/grill/generator/gasoline engine indoors/garage. Camp propane/butane only where manufacturer explicitly permits; open window does not make outdoor-only appliance safe. CO alarms.
PANTRY: grains/oats/rice/pasta; beans/lentils; canned proteins/produce; nut/seed butter; shelf-stable milk; oils; salt/sugar/spices; medical/allergy needs. FIFO, dry/cool/pest-protected. Fats spoil faster than salt/sugar/white rice.
NUTRITION: short term safe water/calories; longer protein, essential fats, vitamins/minerals, fiber, variety. Needs vary by age/work/illness/pregnancy/lactation.

============================================================
08 FIRE, CO, SMOKE, GENERATORS
============================================================
CO is colorless/odorless. Headache, nausea, dizziness, weakness, confusion, chest pain, collapse or several people/pets ill in same enclosed place -> fresh air immediately, emergency care, no re-entry until controlled.
GENERATOR outdoors far from openings/crawlspaces/attached garages; exhaust away; never basement/garage/enclosed porch; weather protection without trapping exhaust; cool before fuel. Never backfeed house through outlet/male-male cord; proper transfer equipment/interlock required.
FIRE: smoke alarms; leave early; close doors if safe; crawl low only as needed; no elevator unless directed; meet outside; stay out. Extinguisher for small contained fire only with escape behind; if not rapidly controlled, leave.
CANDLES: LEDs preferred; if used, stable/attended/away from curtains/bedding/fuel/oxygen/children/pets.
WILDFIRE SMOKE: cleaner indoor air/filtration where safe. N95/P100 reduces particles, not CO/most gases and supplies no oxygen.

============================================================
09 COLD, HEAT, SHELTER
============================================================
COLD: dry layers, block wind/rain, protect head/hands/feet, insulate beneath sleeping people. Smaller safe zone easier to warm.
HYPOTHERMIA: shivering, clumsiness, confusion, slurred speech, drowsiness; severe may stop shivering. Handle gently, remove wet clothing, insulate, warm trunk gradually, help. Warm sweet fluid only if alert/swallowing. No alcohol.
FROSTBITE: protect/refreezing; do not rub/snow. Avoid thaw if unavoidable refreezing likely; medical care.
HEAT: shade, airflow, reduce exertion, fluids, wet/cool skin. Heat exhaustion: sweating/weak/dizzy/headache/nausea. Heat stroke: altered mental status/collapse/seizure/dangerous overheating -> immediate rapid cooling + emergency transport. Remove excess clothing, wet/fan; cold-water immersion can be highly effective if safe/monitored. Do not delay cooling.
SHELTER: avoid flood channels, unstable trees/walls, avalanche/landslide paths, exposed ridges during lightning. Ventilation is necessary for permitted combustion; never makes generator/charcoal indoor-safe.

============================================================
10 ELECTRICITY, BATTERIES, SOLAR
============================================================
Prioritize medical equipment, communications/info, lighting, food preservation. Resistance heating/cooking drains batteries fast.
FORMULAS simple DC/resistive: V=I*R; P=V*I; Wh=W*h. Nominal battery Wh ~V*Ah; usable depends chemistry/rate/temp/age/losses.
12 V/LEAD-ACID: huge short-circuit current; fuse positive near source; avoid jewelry/tools bridging; polarity; ventilation; no sparks/flames.
LITHIUM: stop using swollen/hissing/venting/smoking/hot/severely damaged pack; do not puncture; isolate from combustibles if safe; can reignite.
SOLAR: panel -> compatible charge controller -> battery -> DC load/inverter. Do not connect arbitrary panel to bare lithium cells. Shade reduces output. Correct voltage/chemistry/current.
WIRES/FUSES: undersized wire overheats; fuse protects conductor; long low-voltage runs lose voltage. Do not increase fuse rating just to stop trips.
MAINS: do not infer live-mains safety from low-voltage formulas. Moisture, generators, neutral/ground faults, multiple sources are hidden hazards; de-energize/verify/qualified help.

============================================================
11 COMMUNICATION AND INFORMATION
============================================================
PAPER critical contacts, addresses, meds, meeting points, maps, out-of-area relay.
MESSAGE: TIME / FROM / LOCATION / STATUS / NEED / NEXT ACTION. Exact units/numbers. Preserve source/time through relays.
CELL: brief SMS may pass congestion better than voice. One status message to relay. Reduce brightness/searching; periodically check service.
RADIO: listen first; identify as required; plain concise language; repeat critical numbers; log. FRS short range; GMRS/amateur rules vary; terrain/antenna height/repeaters dominate.
RUMOR: label DIRECT/RELAYED/UNVERIFIED; timestamp; corroborate high-impact claims when safe; send correction on same channel.
PHYSICAL: message board + runners + fixed check times. Message IDs; copies; runner not sole record.

============================================================
12 EVACUATION AND NAVIGATION
============================================================
Evacuate early for orders or clearly worsening fire/flood/chemical/structural threats. Take people, meds, IDs, water, clothes, mobility aids, communications, pets if possible. Do not delay for possessions.
Primary+alternate+walking route. Avoid hazard chokepoints: low road/flood, coast/tsunami, damaged bridges/quake, fire corridors, unstable slopes, underground/flood.
Meeting: near home, outside neighborhood, outside region. Note date/time/destination if leaving message.
Paper map: north/scale/home/water/clinics/shelters/bridges/industrial hazards/high ground/alternates. Mark closures with time/source.
Compass: magnetic vs true/grid north differs; local declination matters for precision; keep away from metal/magnets when reading.
Lost: STOP; last known, route, daylight/weather/water/injury/search. Stay near known route if safe and rescuers expect it. Signal whistle/light/contrast/radio; leave dated note if moving.

============================================================
13 DISASTER-SPECIFIC FAST RULES
============================================================
EARTHQUAKE: Drop, Cover, Hold On; head/neck, away glass. Aftershocks, shoes/gloves, fire/gas/electric, leave serious damage.
FLOOD: high ground early; never moving water; leave basement when rapid flood possible; after assume mud/well/food/electric contamination.
HURRICANE/HIGH WIND: evacuate surge zones; upstairs not guaranteed against surge. Safe nonflooding building -> small interior room away windows; eye calm temporary.
TORNADO: basement/storm shelter or small interior lowest safe floor, away windows; head/neck protection. Manufactured homes vulnerable.
TSUNAMI: strong/long coastal quake or natural/official warning -> immediately inland/uphill; multiple waves hours; first not largest.
WILDFIRE: leave early; mask/hose not fire-front protection; route away smoke/fire; N95 particle only.
LANDSLIDE: move out of channel/path to stable higher ground; burn scar + heavy rain risk.
LIGHTNING: substantial building/hard-topped vehicle; avoid isolated tree/ridge/open water/fence; tent not protection.
WINTER VEHICLE: safe location + expected rescue -> vehicle shelter; clear exhaust before running engine; CO risk.
CHEMICAL: follow shelter/evacuate; close openings/ventilation when ordered; remove/isolate contaminated outer clothing, rinse as appropriate; particle mask not toxic-gas protection.
FALLOUT: substantial building, distance from exterior/roof, dense shielding; remove/bag outer clothing, shower gently; shelter until reliable instructions. Potassium iodide only when authorities recommend for radioactive iodine.
OUTBREAK: ventilation/filtration, stay home sick, reduce close crowded exposure, masks where indicated, hygiene, protect high-risk; current pathogen guidance wins.

============================================================
14 HOUSE SYSTEMS AND BASIC REPAIR
============================================================
Know water main, gas control, electrical main, heating shutoff, sewer cleanout; label tools.
GAS: odor/hissing/damage -> leave/no sparks/contact utility; many systems require qualified restart; never flame-test.
BURST PIPE: safe -> nearest/main shutoff; keep water from energized systems; drain/relieve as appropriate; temporary clamp/patch monitored.
ROOF: interior damage preferable to fall. No wet/icy/windy/dark/damaged-roof improvisation. Catch/move/protect; tarp only safe access/fall protection.
STRUCTURE: major new cracks, lean, sag, foundation shift, fire/partial collapse, ongoing movement/noises -> leave if collapse plausible.
TOOLS: adjustable wrench, pliers, screwdrivers, hammer, hand saw/hacksaw, knife, measure, fasteners, tape, cord/rope, buckets, shovel, pry bar, tarps, gloves, eye protection, headlamp, extinguisher.
LADDER: stable base, correct angle/orientation, three points, no top misuse, no power lines/boxes/chairs.
JACK: jack lifts; stands/cribbing support; never under load on jack alone; chock/stable/correct point.

15 COMMUNITY OPERATIONS
============================================================
Map SKILLS: medical/nursing, first aid, electric, plumbing, mechanics, construction, radio, agriculture, cooking, childcare, elder/disability, languages, logistics, mediation, teaching.
DAILY: people/medical; hazards/weather; water; sanitation; meds; food/fuel/power; communications; work+buddies; next check. Written log.
ZONES: clean food/water, sleeping, sick care, toilets/waste, fuel/chemicals, repair/dust. Mark unsafe.
SECURITY: locks, light, access, neighbor awareness, discretion, buddy travel, de-escalation. Avoid traps/electrified barriers/uncontrolled fire.
RATIONING: measure first; consistent units; publish rules; protect life-critical needs/shared systems; recalc actual burn; secret arbitrary rationing destroys trust.
GOVERNANCE: emergency authority limited/recorded/reviewable; separate custody/audit where possible; rotate roles; decision log/reasons; mediation/appeal; protect vulnerable/minority groups.
HEALTH SURVEILLANCE: track counts/time/location of diarrhea, respiratory illness, fever/rash, injuries, animal die-offs, water failures; protect privacy; clusters may identify source.

============================================================
16 CHILDREN, PREGNANCY, OLDER ADULTS, DISABILITY
============================================================
INFANTS: established breastfeeding reduces water/equipment dependence where possible. Formula exact manufacturer dilution with safe water/clean preparation; never add extra water. Monitor feeding/wet diapers/alertness.
CHILDREN: dehydrate/lose heat faster, smaller medication margins, choking/injury risk; assigned adult; no guessed adult drug doses.
PREGNANCY: hydration/nutrition/infection/heat protection/obstetric access. Heavy bleeding, seizure, severe headache/vision change, severe abdominal pain, breathing difficulty, labor concern, marked reduction established fetal movement -> urgent evaluation.
OLDER: acute confusion can be infection, dehydration, oxygen/glucose/temperature/medication/head injury/stroke; not "just age". Maintain mobility/glasses/hearing/fall prevention.
DISABILITY: ask person what assistance works; preserve mobility aids, AAC, hearing batteries, chargers, service-animal, feeding/continence; accessible evacuation.

============================================================
17 FOOD PRODUCTION AND PRESERVATION
============================================================
Garden is future resilience, not today's calories. Stabilize water/sanitation/shelter first.
CROPS: local season. Mix calorie crops (potato/sweet potato/corn where suitable), legumes, quick greens, roots, squash/storage, herbs, perennials. Diversity reduces single failure.
SOIL: avoid industrial/fuel/sewage/flood unknown/lead debris/fire ash contamination until assessed. Raised clean soil helps but water/dust pathways remain.
FERTILITY: plants need N-P-K plus other nutrients/pH/structure. Organic matter improves water/soil. Excess fertilizer harms/pollutes; test/observe.
COMPOST: plant/food materials, air/moisture, carbon/nitrogen balance. Keep hazardous chemicals, unknown ash, treated wood, pet/human feces out of ordinary food-garden compost.
IRRIGATION: roots, lower evaporation times, drip/mulch; no fecally contaminated water on edible crops.
SEED: cool/dry/dark/labeled crop-variety-year; preserve reserve; open-pollinated more predictable than hybrids but cross-pollination/biennials need species knowledge.
PESTS: identify first; diversity/rotation/sanitation/row covers/hand removal/traps/beneficials/targeted controls. Pesticide label is law/safety; stronger not better.
PRESERVATION: drying/ferment/freezing/water-bath/pressure canning are different. Low-acid food needs validated pressure-canning recipe/altitude/equipment. Never invent time/pressure.

============================================================
18 MECHANICS, MATERIALS, CONSTRUCTION
============================================================
Do not stand under suspended loads; unknown rope/anchor not for people.
LEVER: torque=force*perpendicular distance; longer handle increases torque and break risk.
PULLEY: ideal advantage ~supporting rope segments; friction reduces; more rope travel; anchor loads matter.
ROPES: bowline fixed loop; figure-eight stopper/loop family; clove hitch temporary; two half-hitches secure; trucker's hitch tension. Knots reduce strength; practice; life safety rated gear/training.
FASTENERS: correct driver/wrench; force gradual; multiple critical attachments; tape not sole structure; predrill wood near ends as appropriate; mark disassembly order.
WOOD: moisture rot/mold; splits/knots affect strength; triangles brace; do not remove load-bearing parts blindly.
MASONRY: strong compression, weak tension/out-of-plane; leaning/bulging/cracked damaged walls dangerous.
CONCRETE: strength depends design/mix/compaction/curing/reinforcement; excess water weakens; structural concrete not improvised by generic recipe.
METAL/HOT WORK: eye/face protection/fire watch; never weld/cut unknown sealed containers; coatings can release toxic fumes.
CORROSION: dry/clean/protect; rinse salt; dissimilar metals+electrolyte can galvanically corrode.

============================================================
19 VEHICLES AND TRANSPORT
============================================================
Never idle engine in garage/enclosed area even door open.
FUEL approved containers/lawful quantity/away ignition/living; no mouth siphon; rotate aging fuel.
STRANDED: if safe known road/rescue likely, vehicle gives shelter/visibility; signal/insulate/conserve; snow -> clear tailpipe before engine. Flood/fire/unstable location -> leave.
TIRE: stable away traffic; parking brake/chock; loosen lugs before lift; manufacturer jack point; no body under jack; hand-start; cross/star tighten; final torque spec/recheck.
OVERHEAT: stop safely, shut down, cool; never remove hot radiator cap; inspect only cooled; severe overheating destroys engine.
BICYCLE: tires within markings, brakes function, wheels secure, steering/bearings no dangerous play, chain lightly lubricated/wiped. Find tire cause before new tube.

============================================================
20 WATER SYSTEMS, PLUMBING, VENTILATION
============================================================
HEAD: ~2.31 ft water =1 psi; 10 m ~1 bar/100 kPa. Flow depends pipe diameter/length/roughness/valves/elevation.
SIPHON: filled hose from higher source surface to lower outlet; no energy creation; no mouth-siphon fuel/chemicals.
BACKFLOW: never connect unsafe water to potable plumbing without protection.
FREEZE: protect pipes from cold airflow; maintain heat/drain when appropriate. Never thaw with open flame. Know shutoff because hidden split leaks on thaw.
DRAINS: traps hold water against sewer gas; refill dry trap. Backup -> stop water use/flushing/isolate. No sewer/confined-space entry.
VENTILATION: fresh air controls moisture/disease/pollutants, but smoke/chemical events may call isolation/filtration. Do not block required combustion vents.

22 MATH, UNITS, PHYSICS
============================================================
1 L=1000 mL; 1 kg=1000 g; 1 m=100 cm.
1 gal=3.785 L; 1 qt=0.946 L; 1 lb=0.454 kg; 1 oz mass=28.35 g; 1 in=2.54 cm; 1 ft=0.3048 m; 1 mi=1.609 km; acre=43,560 ft2~4047 m2.
C=(F-32)*5/9; F=C*9/5+32. 0C=32F;20C=68F;37C=98.6F;100C=212F sea level.
Rectangle area=L*W; triangle=b*h/2; circle=pi*r^2; circumference=2*pi*r; rectangular volume=L*W*H; cylinder=pi*r^2*h.
Percent=value/total*100; percent change=(new-old)/old*100; grade%=rise/run*100.
Speed=distance/time; density=mass/volume; pressure=force/area; power=energy/time.
Heat: conduction/convection/radiation. Insulation slows heat flow; wet insulation often poor; wind increases convective loss; evaporation cools, best dry air. Water expands freezing.
Center of gravity beyond support footprint -> tip; heavy items low/wide/sealed.
Energy cannot be created from nothing; efficiency<100%; perpetual-motion claims are error/fraud.

============================================================
23 CHEMICAL SAFETY
============================================================
Keep original labels; never drink bottles. Separate fuels/oxidizers/acids/bases/pesticides from food/water/meds.
Never mix bleach with ammonia, acids/vinegar, toilet cleaners, unknown cleaners; toxic gases form. Bleach degrades/concentration varies; label instructions.
Strong acids/bases burn. Do not neutralize chemical skin injury with opposite reagent; irrigate. Untrained people should avoid concentrated reagents.
Solvents often flammable/toxic; ventilation/no ignition/compatible gloves; never gasoline as cleaner.
Lead-acid battery: acid + hydrogen; lithium: thermal runaway; alkaline leak caustic. Chemistry-specific cleanup/protection.
pH alone never proves drinking water safe.

============================================================
24 WORKSHOP DIAGNOSIS
============================================================
PROCESS: verify complaint -> inspect obvious power/fuel/damage -> isolate subsystem -> measure -> change one variable -> test -> document. Random part swapping consumes spares/creates faults.
DISASSEMBLY: photo/draw, mark orientation, trays/bags, label fasteners, note shims/washers, clean work, preserve seals/gaskets.
LOW-VOLT ELECTRIC: source voltage -> fuse -> connector/corrosion -> switch -> load. No-load voltage may hide high resistance; measure voltage drop under load. Know meter mode; current mode across voltage source can short.
MECHANICAL: locate noise/heat/smell/vibration/leak/play/binding/load dependence; stop before catastrophic damage; compare known-good.
PARTS: label machine/type/dimensions/thread/polarity/voltage; donor record; manuals/diagrams.

25 INFORMATION PRESERVATION AND TEACHING
============================================================
Critical knowledge in multiple forms: printed quick cards, UTF-8 text, standard images/PDF, removable media, distributed copies. Cloud/proprietary-only can vanish.
Plain text durable; CSV tables; PNG/JPEG diagrams; PDF fixed page. README + archive structure + checksums when tools exist.
Index corpus alphabetically/by scenario/by skill/first-hour. Stable section IDs allow radio reference.
LOCAL KNOWLEDGE: wells, shutoffs, bridges, clinics, skilled people, hazards, planting dates, equipment parts, channels, routes. Local often more valuable than generic.
TEACH: purpose -> demonstrate -> learner performs -> correct -> learner teaches. Two people minimum for critical skill. Record competence.
SCIENTIFIC METHOD: observe -> define -> hypothesize -> smallest safe discriminating test -> record -> update. Separate know/think. Preserve failed tests.

============================================================
26 WEATHER AND ENVIRONMENT
============================================================
LIGHTNING: if thunder, substantial building/hard-topped vehicle. Avoid isolated trees/ridges/fields/open water/metal fences; tent not protection.
FLASH FLOOD: rain upstream can flood dry channel; no shelter in washes/culverts/underpasses when storms threaten.
WINTER: avoid travel; stranded safe vehicle often visible shelter; tailpipe clear before periodic engine; CO.
WIND: loose objects become projectiles; avoid windows/trees/unstable facades.
AIR: dust masks/N95 protect particles only; unknown toxic gas or oxygen deficiency requires leaving/special respiratory equipment.

============================================================
27 HYGIENE, DENTAL, SKIN, PESTS
============================================================
FEET: dry, sock change, air footwear, treat hot spots early. Spreading redness/pus/fever/red streak -> infection.
DENTAL: brush safe water/floss. Facial/neck swelling+fever/difficulty swallow/breathe/eye spread -> urgent. No aspirin/caustic chemicals on gum.
SKIN: keep folds dry, change wet clothing, minor cuts clean, avoid shared razors/towels.
RODENTS: secure food/waste; ventilate closed infestations; avoid dry sweeping droppings aerosol; wet/disinfect by public-health guidance, gloves/respiratory protection.
MOSQUITO/TICK: eliminate standing water where possible; nets/repellent/protective clothes; check ticks; local disease guidance.

============================================================
28 STORAGE AND SIX-MONTH PLANNING
============================================================
Do not begin with a giant shopping list. Measure actual household consumption. Build layers: 3 days -> 2 weeks -> 1 month -> longer. Rotate what people eat/use.
WATER long-term storage volume is heavy and often impractical; combine stored buffer with resilient treatment/source plans.
FOOD: calculate calories/protein/fats and dietary constraints but avoid false precision. Test menus using stored food before crisis; include cooking fuel/water. A six-month pantry that requires grid power or huge cooking water is not six-month resilient.
ZONE by frequency: daily kitchen, monthly pantry, deep storage, emergency reserve. Cool/dry/dark/pest-protected. Shelves rated for load; canned/water mass adds quickly.
INVENTORY quarterly: damaged containers, pests/moisture, dates, actual counts, temperature, fuel, medicines, batteries. FIFO.
NONFOOD: soap, toilet supplies, menstrual/continence, diapers, cleaners, trash bags, gloves, filters, lighting, repair consumables, batteries, pet supplies.

============================================================
29 LOCAL FOOD SYSTEM DEVELOPMENT
============================================================
Start small and measure yields. Sun hours, frost dates, water availability, soil, pests, labor decide more than internet yield claims.
Intensive beds/containers/vertical supports can increase usable space but not create sunlight/water/nutrients. Containers dry fast and need drainage.
Season extension: cold frames/row covers/hoop structures can protect crops but overheating/ventilation/wind anchoring matters.
Perennials/orchards take years; plant early in recovery but do not count near-term calories before established.
Livestock adds feed/water/waste/veterinary/predator work. Obtain only with sustainable inputs. Local law/animal welfare/disease control still matter.
Community seed/tool libraries reduce duplication; preserve locally successful varieties and notes, not just seed packets.

============================================================
30 PASSIVE COMFORT, BUILDING ENVELOPE
============================================================
COLD: stop rain/wind leaks first; insulate occupied zone; curtains/blankets safely away heaters; insulation under bodies; sun gain day/cover night.
HEAT: shade outside windows/roof where feasible; close hot-side coverings by day; ventilate when outside cooler; night flush; exhaust hot air high; minimize internal heat; lower/shaded floors.
MOISTURE: cooking/breathing create humidity; condensation -> mold/rot. Vent when outdoor conditions allow. Do not seal combustion venting.
WINDOW BREAK: remove people/glass hazard, gloves/eye protection; temporary board/plastic with secure perimeter, allowance for wind/water drainage; do not create trapped CO/egress hazard.

============================================================
31 COMMUNICATION PROTOCOLS
============================================================
CHECK-IN scheduled windows save battery. Example every even hour; local needs decide.
NUMBER messages sequentially per household/station; acknowledgement by ID prevents duplicates.
PRIORITY: P1 life threat; P2 evacuation/missing/injury; P3 water/medical/infrastructure; P4 welfare/logistics; P5 routine.
RELAY unchanged core text; add RELAY BY/time. Do not silently paraphrase measurements/locations.
LOCATION format standard: street/intersection/building-floor-room or map grid/GPS when available. Repeat digits.
STATUS colors only if community defines them; plain words safer than ambiguous codes.

============================================================
32 SEARCH AND RESCUE BOUNDARIES
============================================================
Untrained people should not enter collapse, fire, confined spaces, swift water, chemical zones, live electrical areas. Mark last-known location, time, clothing, condition, intended route. Search in buddies with map, boundaries, check-ins. Do not create more missing people.
Whistle less energy than shouting. Patterned repeated signal and visual contrast/light can attract searchers. Leave clues before moving.

============================================================
33 WORK SAFETY AND PPE
============================================================
Fatigue, hurry, darkness, dehydration and improvised tools cause injury. Stop when footing/light/attention poor.
EYE protection for hammering/cutting/grinding; hearing around prolonged loud; gloves for sharp/dirty but loose gloves away rotating machinery; respirator matched to hazard.
Knife: stable work, cut away, sheath; dull blade needs force.
Hot work: clear combustibles, extinguisher/fire watch, ventilation, eye/skin protection; no unknown sealed containers.
Dust: wet methods/HEPA where possible; silica/asbestos/lead require special controls, not ordinary dust mask.

============================================================
34 MAINTENANCE
============================================================
Preventive maintenance: inspect, clean, lubricate, tighten, dry, rotate, charge/test, exercise valves/generators where manufacturer allows, replace consumables. Record date/hour meter/condition/action/parts.
Do not over-maintain safety devices: never block pressure relief, oversize fuses, defeat guards/interlocks, tape down switches.
Spare strategy: common failure consumables (filters, belts, fuses, lamps, connectors, seal materials, tire repair, lubricants) chosen for actual equipment, not generic hoard.

============================================================
35 EDUCATION AND ROUTINE
============================================================
Children/adults need routine. Preserve literacy, arithmetic, practical science, history/records, vocational skill. Short daily lessons and apprentice work prevent knowledge loss.
For each system create one-page: purpose; normal state; shutdown; restart; hazards; daily/weekly/monthly checks; spare parts; owner+backup.
Teach by performance, not reading: learner demonstrates under supervision then teaches another.

============================================================
36 COMPUTING AND DIGITAL FALLBACK
============================================================
Conserve functioning devices for high-value tasks: records/search/maps/calculation/comms. Keep chargers/cables/adapters labeled. Prefer local/offline files over account-dependent cloud.
Offline archive should have START.txt, keyword index, simple directory names, standard formats, duplicate media. Test on more than one device/OS.
If one device can host local Wi-Fi/web server, browsers across mixed devices can access common library without app installs; but retain direct copies because server is single point.
Power-cycle less when boot risk/storage corruption; make backups before experiments. Do not erase a working device to install untested system during crisis.

============================================================
37 SALVAGE AND CANNIBALIZATION
============================================================
Before dismantling: is donor repairable/useful? Is needed part obtainable elsewhere? Photograph/label. Preserve screws/connectors/cables/motors/switches/bearings/sensors only if recognizable and safely stored. Unknown lithium cells, capacitors, CRT/high-voltage gear, refrigerants, pressurized cylinders require caution.
Cannibalize lowest-value failed assets before functioning multipurpose equipment. Keep donor log so future repairers know what disappeared.

============================================================
38 ENERGY BUDGET
============================================================
List loads: watts, hours/day, Wh/day, criticality, surge. Sum critical first. Example:
radio 5W*4h=20Wh; phone charge ~15Wh; LED 5W*5h=25Wh; medical device label-specific. Compare with realistic battery usable Wh and daily solar/generator input.
Energy hierarchy: eliminate load -> shorten runtime -> lower-power substitute -> schedule/batch -> generate/store more. Efficiency is often cheaper than generation.
Fuel generator efficiency worsens at tiny/poorly matched load; batch charging may conserve fuel where equipment permits. Never compromise CO/exhaust safety for efficiency.

============================================================
39 WATER BUDGET
============================================================
Separate DRINK/COOK, MEDICAL, HAND/HYGIENE, DISH, LAUNDRY/CLEANING. Measure container volumes and actual daily use. Fix leaks. Use nonpotable water only for appropriate tasks and label clearly. Do not use contaminated graywater on wounds/food-contact surfaces.
Source resilience: stored buffer + treatment method + collection/resupply plan + clean containers. One giant tank is a single failure; multiple containers improve portability/redundancy.

============================================================
40 FOOD BUDGET
============================================================
Inventory by calories/protein/fat, but also fuel/water/prep. Use perishables first while safe. Reserve no-cook food for evacuation/fuel failure. Track servings/day; do not let snack foods silently consume reserve.
Test a 3-day stored-food menu during normal time: measure water, fuel, cleanup, waste, acceptance. Correct plan from evidence.

============================================================
41 MEDICAL RED-FLAG CARD
============================================================
URGENT/EMERGENCY where possible: severe breathing trouble; unresponsive/not breathing normally; uncontrolled major bleeding; stroke signs; severe chest pain/possible heart attack; anaphylaxis; prolonged/repeated seizure; major head/spine trauma with neurologic change; severe burn/electrical/chemical inhalation; poisoning; severe dehydration/shock; pregnancy heavy bleeding/seizure/severe symptoms; infant severe lethargy/breathing/dehydration; rapidly spreading infection with systemic illness.
When professional care unavailable: stabilize airway/breathing/bleeding, prevent temperature extremes, safe fluids only when appropriate, document timing/treatments, arrange transport/communication. Do not let an improvised advanced procedure delay reachable skilled care.

============================================================
42 FOOD-SAFETY DECISION CARD
============================================================
Was food shelf-stable and package intact? Usually keep.
Perishable continuously <=40F/4C? Usually keep.
Temperature/time unknown after long outage, high-risk food? Discard.
Bulging/leaking/damaged can/jar? Discard without tasting.
Floodwater contacted food package? Discard porous/cardboard and many compromised containers; follow official guidance for intact commercial cans when available.
Mold? Rules vary by food; soft/high-moisture foods generally discard rather than trimming; uncertain -> discard.
Chemical contamination/smoke residues? Do not assume washing makes safe.

============================================================
43 WATER DECISION CARD
============================================================
Sealed known safe -> drink/store.
Unknown clear natural source, no chemical suspicion -> treat biologically.
Cloudy -> settle/prefilter then disinfect.
Saltwater -> needs desalination, not ordinary filter/boil.
Fuel/solvent/pesticide/industrial/firefighting contamination -> find another source/specialized treatment.
Floodwater -> avoid; chemical+microbial risk.
Fallout particles -> sealed source best; ordinary boiling not radiation removal.

============================================================
44 REPAIR DECISION CARD
============================================================
Can hazard be isolated/de-energized/depressurized? If no, stop.
Does job involve height, collapse, confined space, live mains, gas, high pressure, unknown chemical, high-voltage battery? Seek trained help/defer.
Can temporary repair fail safely/visibly? If yes, consider.
Document temporary status; schedule inspection; do not hide with cosmetic cover.
Test at reduced load first when possible.

50 LOW-TECH FALLBACK
============================================================
No internet -> SMS/radio/runner/bulletin.
No phones -> radio/whistle/runner/paper.
No GPS -> paper map/compass/landmarks.
No electronic inventory -> cards/ledger/tallies.
No electric light -> daylight scheduling/LED battery; flame only controlled.
No pump -> gravity/manual collection where safe.
No refrigeration -> shelf-stable food, safe immediate use/preservation.
No electronic payment -> cash/paper ledger.
No printer -> hand-copy priority cards; assign proofreader.
No calculator -> written arithmetic/unit analysis.
The objective is graceful degradation, not preserving a particular device.

============================================================
51 REFERENCE FORMULAS
============================================================
Water mass: kg ~= liters; lb ~= gallons*8.34.
Tank rectangular liters = L(m)*W(m)*H(m)*1000.
Cylinder liters ~= pi*r(m)^2*h(m)*1000.
Electrical: W=V*A; Wh=W*h; A=W/V.
Battery approximate Wh=nominal V*Ah (usable less).
Mechanical: work=force*distance; power=work/time; torque=force*lever arm; pressure=force/area.
Flow average=volume/time.
Days supply=stock/daily use.
Cost/unit=total cost/usable units.
Map real distance=map distance*scale.
Do dimensional/unit checks.

============================================================
52 PROBLEM-SOLVING PROTOCOL
============================================================
1 define desired state;
2 make scene/system safe;
3 observe before changing;
4 list plausible causes ranked simple/common/dangerous;
5 test one discriminating variable safely;
6 record;
7 repair smallest confirmed cause;
8 test under controlled load;
9 monitor recurrence;
10 update documentation.
Avoid simultaneous changes because success/failure becomes uninterpretable.

============================================================
53 COMMUNITY CONFLICT RULES
============================================================
State facts and uncertainty. Separate person from problem. Use neutral mediator. Record resource rules before scarcity becomes acute. Decisions affecting many need more than one custodian/approver where feasible. No punishment without evidence/process. Correct public errors publicly. Private grievances not broadcast as hazard reports.

============================================================
54 AFTER-ACTION REVIEW
============================================================
Within safe period ask: What happened? What was expected? What worked? What failed? Near misses? Which supplies unused/critical? Which information late/wrong? Which single points failed? What training missing? Actions, owner, due date. Preserve review with archive; change plans rather than merely praising effort.

============================================================
55 PRE-EVENT HIGH-RETURN PREPARATION
============================================================
Learn CPR/AED/bleeding in person. Learn water/gas/electric controls. Maintain smoke/CO/extinguisher. Practice evacuation/communication. Paper maps/contacts. Medication buffer legally/medically feasible. Rotate food/water. Backup docs offline. Maintain vehicle/tools/batteries. Know neighbors.
BASIC CACHE: water/containers/treatment; familiar shelf-stable food/opener; essential meds/list; first-aid/bleeding; headlamps; batteries/power bank; radio; smoke/CO alarms; extinguisher; seasonal clothes/blankets; sanitation bags/bucket/soap; gloves/eye/N95; tools/tarps/cord/tape; maps/contacts/cash; chargers; infant/pet/disability items.

============================================================
56 FINAL DECISION RULES
============================================================
1 Avoid the second casualty.
2 Fast fatal threats first: scene, bleeding, breathing, fire/CO, drowning, lethal temperature.
3 Safe water+sanitation outrank elaborate food acquisition.
4 Keep clean and dirty physically separate.
5 Smell/taste cannot prove safety for CO, microbes, chemicals, spoiled food.
6 Invisible hazards: electricity, CO, structural instability, flood contamination, low oxygen.
7 Property damage is preferable to risky roof/live wire/confined space/flood/fire entry.
8 Timestamp observations; separate evidence from rumor.
9 Redundancy beats one perfect tool.
10 Under context-sensitive precision, stabilize/isolate/preserve options/seek qualified help rather than confident improvisation.

#  Community Resilience Building - Network Development Guide

##  Overview
Individual preparedness has limits. True long-term resilience requires strong community networks that can coordinate resources, share knowledge, and provide mutual support during extended disruptions. This guide provides frameworks for building and maintaining resilient community networks.

##  Community Network Types

### **Primary Networks (Core Survival)**

#### **Neighborhood Circles (15-25 People)**
**Optimal Size**: 20 people (5-6 households)
**Geographic Range**: 2-3 blocks or 0.5 mile radius
**Meeting Frequency**: Weekly during normal times, daily during crises

##### **Core Functions**
- **Immediate Response**: First 24-48 hours of crisis
- **Resource Sharing**: Daily needs and emergency supplies
- **Security**: Neighborhood watch and protection
- **Communication Hub**: Information relay point

##### **Organization Structure**
- **Circle Coordinator**: Rotating monthly role
- **Resource Manager**: Tracks shared supplies
- **Communications Lead**: Maintains contact systems
- **Security Coordinator**: Organizes watch schedules

##### **Formation Steps**
1. **Week 1-2**: Identify interested neighbors
2. **Week 3-4**: First informal gathering, gauge interest
3. **Month 2**: Establish regular meeting schedule
4. **Month 3**: Define roles and responsibilities
5. **Month 4+**: Begin skill sharing and resource mapping

#### **Skill Circles (10-15 People)**
**Focus**: Specific skill development and sharing
**Meeting Frequency**: Bi-weekly workshops/practice sessions

##### **Medical Circle**
- **Participants**: Medical professionals, first aid trained
- **Resources**: Medical supplies, knowledge sharing
- **Training**: CPR, first aid, emergency medicine
- **Equipment**: Shared medical kit, emergency supplies

##### **Food Production Circle**
- **Participants**: Gardeners, farmers, food preservers
- **Resources**: Seeds, tools, preservation equipment
- **Training**: Gardening, preservation, nutrition
- **Equipment**: Shared tools, processing equipment

##### **Technical Circle**
- **Participants**: Mechanics, electricians, engineers
- **Resources**: Tools, repair knowledge, parts
- **Training**: Repair skills, alternative energy
- **Equipment**: Shared tool library, workshop space

##### **Security Circle**
- **Participants**: Military, police, security-minded civilians
- **Resources**: Training, equipment, coordination
- **Training**: Self-defense, situational awareness
- **Equipment**: Communication gear, basic protection

### **Secondary Networks (Extended Support)**

#### **Resource Hubs (50-100 People)**
**Geographic Range**: 1-2 mile radius
**Function**: Larger resource pooling and distribution

##### **Community Resource Center**
- **Location**: Church, school, community center
- **Storage**: Bulk supplies, shared equipment
- **Distribution**: Coordinated resource sharing
- **Governance**: Representative council from neighborhoods

##### **Tool Library System**
- **Inventory**: 200-500 tools and equipment pieces
- **Check-out System**: Borrowing protocols
- **Maintenance**: Repair and replacement fund
- **Training**: Tool use and safety workshops

##### **Seed Library**
- **Collection**: Locally adapted varieties
- **Exchange**: Annual seed swap events
- **Education**: Growing and seed saving workshops
- **Preservation**: Climate-controlled storage

#### **Economic Cooperatives (30-75 People)**
**Focus**: Alternative economic systems during disruption

##### **Buying Cooperative**
- **Bulk Purchasing**: Food, fuel, supplies at wholesale
- **Cost Savings**: 20-40% savings through volume
- **Quality Control**: Vetting of suppliers and products
- **Distribution**: Coordinated pickup and delivery

##### **Skill Exchange Network**
- **Time Banking**: Hour-for-hour skill trading
- **Service Directory**: Available skills and services
- **Quality Assurance**: Feedback and rating system
- **Coordination**: Central scheduling system

##### **Local Currency System**
- **Currency Design**: Community-specific exchange medium
- **Acceptance Network**: Local businesses and individuals
- **Backing System**: Hours, goods, or services backing
- **Administration**: Elected oversight committee

##  Network Building Process

### **Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1-3)**

#### **Community Assessment**
**Goal**: Map existing networks and identify key people

##### **Neighborhood Mapping Exercise**
1. **Door-to-Door Survey**: Introduce yourself, gauge interest
2. **Skills Inventory**: What can each person contribute?
3. **Resource Mapping**: What does each household have to share?
4. **Interest Assessment**: Willingness to participate level
5. **Leadership Identification**: Natural leaders and organizers

##### **Asset Mapping**
- **Physical Assets**: Buildings, tools, equipment, land
- **Human Assets**: Skills, knowledge, connections
- **Institutional Assets**: Churches, schools, businesses
- **Natural Assets**: Water sources, growing spaces

##### **Communication Survey**
- **Preferred Methods**: Phone, email, text, in-person
- **Technology Access**: Internet, smartphones, radios
- **Backup Systems**: Alternative communication plans
- **Information Preferences**: Meeting style, frequency

#### **Initial Network Formation**
**Goal**: Establish first working groups

##### **Introductory Community Meeting**
- **Venue**: Neutral, accessible location
- **Agenda**: Introductions, purpose discussion, next steps
- **Outcome**: Interest groups formation
- **Follow-up**: Contact information exchange

##### **Working Groups Establishment**
- **Communication Group**: Newsletter, phone tree, bulletin board
- **Skills Group**: Workshops, training coordination
- **Resources Group**: Sharing systems, bulk buying
- **Emergency Group**: Preparedness planning, response protocols

### **Phase 2: Development (Months 4-12)**

#### **System Implementation**
**Goal**: Put working systems in place

##### **Communication Systems**
1. **Phone Tree**: Rapid information dissemination
2. **Newsletter**: Monthly community updates
3. **Bulletin Boards**: Physical information posting
4. **Digital Groups**: Email lists, social media groups
5. **Emergency Protocols**: Crisis communication procedures

##### **Resource Sharing Systems**
1. **Tool Library**: Check-out system and storage
2. **Bulk Buying**: Quarterly group orders
3. **Skill Exchange**: Time bank or trading system
4. **Emergency Cache**: Community emergency supplies
5. **Transportation**: Ride sharing, emergency transport

##### **Regular Activities**
- **Monthly Meetings**: Community updates and planning
- **Skill Workshops**: Monthly skill-sharing sessions
- **Social Events**: Quarterly community building events
- **Emergency Drills**: Annual preparedness exercises

#### **Leadership Development**
**Goal**: Develop sustainable leadership structure

##### **Rotating Leadership**
- **Term Length**: 6-month terms for key roles
- **Succession Planning**: Leadership pipeline development
- **Training Programs**: Leadership skill development
- **Mentorship**: Experienced leaders guide new ones

##### **Committee Structure**
- **Steering Committee**: Overall coordination (5-7 people)
- **Functional Committees**: Specific area focus (3-5 people each)
- **Working Groups**: Task-specific teams (varies)
- **Advisory Council**: Experienced advisors (no voting)

### **Phase 3: Maturation (Year 2+)**

#### **Network Expansion**
**Goal**: Connect with broader networks

##### **Inter-Community Connections**
- **Neighboring Networks**: Exchange visits and coordination
- **Regional Networks**: Broader area coordination
- **Specialized Networks**: Connect to expertise networks
- **Mutual Aid Agreements**: Formal support agreements

##### **Institutional Partnerships**
- **Local Government**: Emergency management coordination
- **Businesses**: Resource and service partnerships
- **Non-profits**: Skill and resource sharing
- **Educational Institutions**: Training partnerships

#### **Advanced Capabilities**
**Goal**: Develop sophisticated response capabilities

##### **Emergency Response Teams**
- **Medical Response**: First aid and triage capabilities
- **Search and Rescue**: Trained search teams
- **Communications**: Ham radio and backup systems
- **Logistics**: Resource coordination and distribution

##### **Alternative Systems**
- **Local Currency**: Full alternative economy
- **Local Production**: Food and goods production
- **Energy Systems**: Community renewable energy
- **Governance**: Consensus decision-making systems

##  Network Health Metrics

### **Participation Metrics**

#### **Engagement Levels**
- **Core Participants**: 10-15% of community (daily engagement)
- **Active Participants**: 25-35% of community (weekly engagement)
- **Occasional Participants**: 40-50% of community (monthly engagement)
- **Aware Non-participants**: 60-70% know about network

#### **Activity Participation**
- **Meeting Attendance**: 40-60% of active participants
- **Skill Workshops**: 60-80% of interested participants
- **Social Events**: 70-90% of community members
- **Emergency Drills**: 50-70% of community members

### **Functionality Metrics**

#### **Communication Effectiveness**
- **Information Reach**: 90%+ of community in 24 hours
- **Accuracy Rate**: 95%+ of information accurate
- **Response Time**: <2 hours for non-emergency information
- **Feedback Loop**: 80%+ of messages acknowledge receipt

#### **Resource Sharing**
- **Tool Library Usage**: 60%+ utilization rate
- **Skill Exchange**: 20+ hours per month community-wide
- **Bulk Buying**: 30%+ participation rate
- **Emergency Response**: <1 hour mobilization time

##  Trust Building Strategies

### **Social Capital Development**

#### **Incremental Trust Building**
1. **Low-Risk Interactions**: Social events, information sharing
2. **Small Favors**: Minor assistance, skill sharing
3. **Shared Projects**: Group activities with common goals
4. **Crisis Response**: Support during small emergencies
5. **Long-term Commitment**: Multi-year relationship building

#### **Trust Indicators**
- **Resource Sharing**: Willingness to lend valuable items
- **Information Sharing**: Open communication about challenges
- **Conflict Resolution**: Ability to resolve disputes internally
- **Leadership Acceptance**: Community support for leaders
- **Mutual Support**: Helping without expectation of return

### **Conflict Resolution Systems**

#### **Prevention Strategies**
- **Clear Expectations**: Written agreements and protocols
- **Regular Check-ins**: Monthly satisfaction surveys
- **Early Intervention**: Address issues before escalation
- **Mediation Training**: Multiple trained mediators available

#### **Resolution Process**
1. **Direct Communication**: Encourage direct resolution
2. **Peer Mediation**: Neutral community member mediation
3. **Committee Review**: Formal committee assessment
4. **Community Decision**: Full community involvement if needed
5. **External Mediation**: Professional mediation if required

##  Emergency Response Protocols

### **Communication Cascade**

#### **Level 1: Neighborhood Alert**
- **Trigger**: Local emergency or concern
- **Method**: Phone tree, door-to-door
- **Timeline**: 30 minutes for full neighborhood
- **Response**: Neighborhood coordinator assessment

#### **Level 2: Community Mobilization**
- **Trigger**: Significant local emergency
- **Method**: All communication channels
- **Timeline**: 2 hours for full community
- **Response**: Community emergency response activation

#### **Level 3: Regional Coordination**
- **Trigger**: Area-wide emergency
- **Method**: Ham radio, emergency networks
- **Timeline**: 4 hours for regional coordination
- **Response**: Mutual aid agreements activation

### **Resource Mobilization**

#### **Emergency Resource Pool**
- **Location**: Secure, accessible community storage
- **Contents**: 72-hour supplies for 20% of community
- **Access**: Emergency coordinator key holders
- **Replenishment**: Quarterly inventory and restocking

#### **Skill Mobilization**
- **Medical Team**: Respond within 15 minutes
- **Technical Team**: Infrastructure assessment and repair
- **Communications Team**: Information management
- **Logistics Team**: Resource coordination and distribution

##  Funding and Sustainability

### **Financial Models**

#### **Membership Dues**
- **Structure**: Sliding scale based on ability to pay
- **Amount**: $20-50 per household annually
- **Uses**: Communication, meeting space, emergency supplies
- **Administration**: Elected treasurer oversight

#### **Activity Fees**
- **Skill Workshops**: $5-15 per workshop

# Urban Power & Energy Guide

This guide covers the essentials of backup power solutions and managing your energy consumption during a power outage.

## 1. Backup Power Solutions

Having a way to generate your own electricity is a major advantage in a disaster. Here are the most common options, from simplest to most complex.

### Tier 1: Portable Power Banks

*   **What they are:** Small, portable batteries for charging phones, tablets, and other USB-powered devices.
*   **Pros:** Inexpensive, easy to use, portable.
*   **Cons:** Limited capacity. They won't power large appliances.
*   **Best For:** Keeping your communication devices charged.

### Tier 2: Portable Power Stations (Solar Generators)

*   **What they are:** Large, rechargeable battery packs with multiple outlets (AC, DC, USB). They can be recharged via a wall outlet, a car, or solar panels.
*   **Pros:** Silent, no fumes (safe for indoor use), multiple charging options.
*   **Cons:** More expensive than power banks, limited capacity (you can't run a refrigerator for days on end).
*   **Best For:** Running small, essential devices like a laptop, a CPAP machine, or a few lights.

### Tier 3: Gasoline Generators

*   **What they are:** The traditional solution for backup power.
*   **Pros:** Powerful (can run large appliances like refrigerators and freezers), relatively inexpensive for the amount of power they provide.
*   **Cons:** **NEVER USE INDOORS.** They produce deadly carbon monoxide. They are loud, require a steady supply of gasoline (which can be hard to find in a disaster), and require regular maintenance.
*   **Best For:** Powering essential large appliances.

### Tier 4: Whole-Home Generators / Solar with Battery Backup

*   **What they are:** Professionally installed systems that can power your entire home.
*   **Pros:** Seamless, automatic power. High capacity.
*   **Cons:** Very expensive.
*   **Best For:** Long-term, whole-home power solutions.

## 2. Energy Management: Powering Down

During an outage, you need to be strategic about your energy use. Your goal is to make your stored power last as long as possible.

*   **Know Your Needs:** Before a disaster, figure out which of your devices are essential. Make a list of their power requirements (in watts).
*   **Turn Things Off:** Unplug any device you are not actively using. Even in standby mode, many electronics draw a small amount of power (this is called a "phantom load").
*   **Use Manual Alternatives:** Use a hand-crank can opener instead of an electric one. Use a manual coffee grinder. Look for non-electric alternatives for your daily tasks.
*   **Lighting:** Use flashlights, headlamps, or solar-powered lanterns for light. Avoid using the lights in your home, as they are a significant power drain.
*   **Heating and Cooling:** Your home's heating and cooling systems are likely your biggest energy consumers. Do not attempt to run them on a portable generator. For warmth, focus on heating a single, small room and use blankets and warm clothing. For cooling, use passive methods like opening windows at night and closing them during the day.

By having a modest backup power solution and being mindful of your energy consumption, you can maintain a degree of normalcy and safety during a power outage.

# Urban Communications Guide

When a disaster strikes, communication networks are often the first casualty. This guide covers how to create a family communication plan and the basics of using radios to stay connected.

## 1. Family Emergency Communication Plan

A simple, clear plan is essential for reconnecting with loved ones.

*   **Contact Information:**
    *   Create a physical, waterproof list of all important phone numbers. Don't rely on your phone's contact list.
    *   Include an **Out-of-Town Contact**. It is often easier to make long-distance calls than local ones during a regional disaster. This person can act as a central point of contact for your family.
*   **Meeting Places:** Designate three specific locations:
    1.  **Neighborhood Meeting Place:** A safe spot near your home (e.g., a specific tree, a neighbor's porch).
    2.  **Regional Meeting Place:** A location outside your immediate neighborhood, in case you can't get home (e.g., a library, a friend's house).
    3.  **Out-of-Town Meeting Place:** A location in another city or state, in case of a large-scale evacuation.
*   **Communication Methods:**
    *   **Text Messages First:** Text messages (SMS) often get through when voice calls fail because they use less network bandwidth.
    *   **If You Must Call:** Keep calls brief to conserve your phone's battery and free up the network for others.
*   **Practice the Plan:** Run through your plan with your family. Make sure everyone knows the meeting places and has the contact information.

**Action Item:** Fill out a family communication plan. You can find excellent, free templates online from sources like Ready.gov.

## 2. Two-Way Radio Communication

When cell phones and the internet are down, radios are your best bet for staying in touch.

*   **FRS (Family Radio Service):**
    *   **License:** None required.
    *   **Power:** Low power (max 2 watts).
    *   **Range:** Short-range (typically 0.5 to 2 miles).
    *   **Best For:** Communicating with family members within a small area (e.g., your neighborhood).
    *   **Notes:** These are the common "walkie-talkies" you can buy in most stores. They are simple to use and a great starting point.

*   **GMRS (General Mobile Radio Service):**
    *   **License:** Required (one license covers your entire immediate family, no test required).
    *   **Power:** Higher power (up to 50 watts).
    *   **Range:** Medium-range (typically 5 to 25 miles).
    *   **Best For:** Communicating over longer distances, like across a town.
    *   **Notes:** GMRS radios can use repeaters, which can significantly extend their range.

*   **Ham Radio (Amateur Radio):**
    *   **License:** Required (must pass a test).
    *   **Power:** High power.
    *   **Range:** Long-range (can be global).
    *   **Best For:** Communicating over very long distances, and for hobbyists who want to be part of a larger community.
    *   **Notes:** Ham radio is the most powerful and versatile option, but it also has the highest barrier to entry.

### Radio Etiquette:

*   **Listen First:** Before you transmit, listen to make sure the channel is clear.
*   **Keep it Brief:** Be concise and to the point.
*   **Identify Yourself:** Use your call sign (for GMRS and Ham) or a nickname.
*   **Speak Clearly:** Use plain language. Avoid jargon.

By having a solid family communication plan and a set of two-way radios, you can dramatically improve your ability to stay connected and informed during an emergency.

# Post-Disaster Home Repair Guide

This guide covers the most critical first steps for securing your home and preventing further damage after a disaster.

## 1. Safety First: When in Doubt, Get Out

Before you even think about repairs, assess the structural safety of your home.

*   **Look for:**
    *   Large cracks in the foundation or walls.
    *   Sagging ceilings or roof.
    *   The smell of gas or burning insulation.
*   If you see any of these signs, **do not enter**. Have the home inspected by a professional when it is safe to do so.

## 2. Utility Shut-Offs

Knowing how to shut off your utilities is one of the most important post-disaster skills.

*   **When to Shut it Off:** If you see sparks, frayed wires, the smell of burning insulation, or if your home has been flooded.
*   **How to Shut it Off:**
    1.  Locate your main electrical panel (usually in the garage, basement, or an outside closet).
    2.  Turn off all the individual circuit breakers first.
    3.  Flip the main breaker (the large one at the top) to the "Off" position.

*   **When to Shut it Off:** **Only if you smell gas, hear a hissing sound, or see a broken gas line.**
*   **How to Shut it Off:**
    1.  Locate your gas meter on the exterior of your house.
    2.  Use a crescent wrench or a specialized gas shut-off tool to turn the valve a quarter turn until it is perpendicular to the pipe.
    3.  **NEVER** attempt to turn the gas back on yourself. This must be done by a qualified professional.

*   **When to Shut it Off:** If you have a burst pipe or if local authorities have issued a boil water alert.
*   **How to Shut it Off:**
    1.  Locate your home's main water shut-off valve. This is usually where the main water pipe enters your house, often in a basement or on an exterior wall.
    2.  Turn the valve clockwise to close it.

## 3. Securing Your Home

*   **Board Up Windows:** Use plywood or other sturdy material to cover broken windows. This will protect your home from the elements and from intruders.
*   **Tarp Your Roof:** If your roof is damaged, a securely fastened tarp can prevent water from getting into your home.
*   **Document Everything:** Before you make any repairs, take photos and videos of all the damage for your insurance claim.

This guide covers the immediate, critical steps. For more complex repairs, always consult a professional when it is safe to do so.

##  Quality Control Systems

### **Monthly Inspection Protocol**

#### **Visual Inspection Checklist**
- [ ] **Containers**: Check for damage, pest intrusion
- [ ] **Labels**: Verify dates, contents accuracy
- [ ] **Environment**: Temperature, humidity readings
- [ ] **Organization**: FIFO rotation compliance

#### **Quality Indicators**
| Food Type | Check For | Action If Found |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Canned Goods** | Bulging, rust, dents | Discard immediately |
| **Dried Foods** | Moisture, insects | Repackage or discard |
| **Grains** | Weevils, rancidity | Freezer treatment or discard |
| **Oils** | Rancid smell, cloudiness | Replace immediately |

### **Quarterly Deep Assessment**
- **Inventory Reconciliation**: Compare actual vs. recorded
- **Nutritional Analysis**: Ensure balanced diet capability
- **Shelf Life Review**: Identify items approaching expiration
- **System Improvements**: Identify storage or rotation issues
