ARKOFTHEDUCK FIELD MANUAL (100K TIER)
Plain-language operating manual for ordinary people during severe, prolonged
infrastructure failure. This is preparedness guidance, NOT a substitute for
professionals. Whenever real emergency services, clinicians, or official
instructions are available, they take priority over this document.

=== HOW TO USE THIS ===
Skim the INDEX. In a crisis, jump to the numbered section you need. Each
section leads with the single most important action. When a detail is
context-sensitive and could be dangerous if wrong, this manual gives a
principle and a "when to escalate" rule instead of false precision.

=== 1. MINDSET AND THE ORDER OF SURVIVAL ===
Panic kills. Stop, breathe, look, plan, act. Adopt "protect life first, then
capability." Work the ORDER OF LIFE and never skip up the list to comfort while
a higher threat is unmet:
- Air: minutes. Blocked airway, smoke, toxic gas, drowning.
- Severe bleeding and body-core temperature: minutes to an hour.
- Water: about 3 days without.
- Food: about 3 weeks without.
- Morale/social order: degrades over days and can kill through bad decisions.
Assign roles early (water, security, medical, sanitation, comms). Keep the
group together, warm, dry, hydrated, informed. Rest and ration effort; injuries
and mistakes rise with exhaustion. Write things down; memory fails under stress.

=== 2. IMMEDIATE SCENE SAFETY ===
Do not become the next casualty. Before helping others or entering a structure:
- Look up, down, around: fire, smoke, downed power lines, gas smell, unstable
  walls/roof, rising or moving water, sharp debris, traffic.
- If you smell gas, hear hissing, or see leaning structures or floodwater
  rising: leave now, move uphill/upwind, do not use switches, flames, or engines.
- Turn off utilities if you know how and it is safe: gas at the meter (only if
  you smell gas; needs a wrench; once off, a pro must turn it back on),
  electricity at the main breaker, water at the main valve.
- Assume downed lines are live. Stay back at least the length of a bus. If a
  line touches your car, stay inside unless fire forces you out; then jump clear
  landing with feet together, and shuffle away without touching car and ground
  at once.

=== 3. WATER ===
You need roughly 2-4 liters per person per day for drinking, more for heat,
work, illness, or nursing. Dehydration degrades judgment fast.
FIND: stored water, water heater tank, toilet tank (not bowl; not if chemical
tabs used), pipes, canned vegetable liquid, rain, dew, streams, ponds, snow
(melt before eating - eating snow chills you and wastes energy).
MAKE IT SAFE - two steps:
1) CLEAR it: let sediment settle, then pour through a clean cloth or sand/cloth
   filter. Clearer water disinfects better.
2) DISINFECT (kills germs; clearing alone does not):
   - BOIL (best): bring to a rolling boil 1 minute; 3 minutes above ~2000 m
     (6500 ft). Cover, cool, store.
   - BLEACH: plain unscented household bleach (~5-6% sodium hypochlorite). Add
     about 2 drops per liter (8 drops per gallon), double if cloudy/cold, stir,
     wait 30 minutes; a faint chlorine smell should remain. If not, repeat once.
   - SOLAR (SODIS): clear PET bottle, clear water, full sun 6 hours (2 days if
     cloudy). Last resort.
   - Commercial filters/tablets: follow their label.
NOTE: Boiling and bleach kill microbes but do NOT remove chemical/fuel/heavy
contamination. If water may be chemically polluted (industrial, floodwater
through chemicals), do not drink it; find another source. Distillation removes
salt and many chemicals but not all; it is a last resort for salt/brackish water.
STORE: clean, food-safe, covered containers; keep off the floor; label; rotate.

=== 4. FOOD ===
Ration from day one; you rarely know how long. Eat perishable/fridge food
first, then freezer, then shelf-stable. A fridge holds ~4 h closed; a full
freezer ~48 h. "When in doubt, throw it out" - but do not waste safe food.
SAFETY: discard food that touched floodwater, is warm and was previously
refrigerated for >2 h, smells or looks wrong, or is in swollen/leaking/rusted
cans. You cannot see or smell all toxins; err toward caution with meat, dairy,
eggs, cooked rice.
NUTRITION: prioritize calories and salts when active or hot. Do not force food
if water is scarce - digestion uses water. Sprouting seeds/beans adds vitamins.
INFANTS: breastfeeding is safest in crisis; if using formula, use safe water.

=== 5. WARMTH, COLD, HEAT, SHELTER ===
Core temperature is a life threat. Wet + wind + still air steal heat fastest.
COLD: stay DRY; wet clothing loses most of its insulation. Layer: wicking base,
insulating mid, wind/water shell. Cover head, neck, hands, feet. Insulate from
the ground (that is where you lose heat) with anything: cardboard, leaves,
packs. Make shelter small so your body heat fills it. Share warmth. Move to
generate heat but do not sweat.
HYPOTHERMIA signs: intense shivering, then clumsiness, confusion, slurred
speech, then shivering STOPS (danger). Warm gently: dry them, insulate, skin-to-
skin, warm sweet drinks if fully alert. Handle gently; do not rub limbs.
HEAT: rest in shade, loosen clothing, wet skin and fan, drink water with a
little salt/food. HEATSTROKE (hot skin, confusion, collapse, may stop sweating)
is deadly: cool aggressively NOW - water, wet cloths, fan, shade, immerse if
possible - and get help.
SHELTER PRIORITIES: dry, out of wind, insulated from ground, ventilated (see
carbon monoxide), defensible from water/fire, near water and signaling.

=== 6. AIR, CARBON MONOXIDE, FIRE ===
CARBON MONOXIDE (CO) is the most common post-disaster killer after the event.
It is invisible and odorless. NEVER run generators, engines, charcoal, gas
grills, camp stoves, or unvented fuel heaters inside a home, tent, garage, or
near windows. Generators go OUTSIDE, far from and downwind of openings.
Symptoms: headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness, confusion; multiple people or
pets sick at once. Response: get everyone to fresh air immediately, then seek
help. A battery CO alarm is cheap lifesaving gear.
FIRE - prevent and fight small, flee big:
- Keep flames away from fuel, fabric, and sleeping people. Never leave open
  flame unattended.
- Small fire: smother (lid, dirt, blanket), or use an extinguisher with PASS -
  Pull pin, Aim at base, Squeeze, Sweep. Grease fire: cover, cut heat, never
  water. Electrical: cut power, do not use water.
- If it is spreading or filling a room with smoke, GET OUT and stay out. Crawl
  low under smoke. Feel doors with the back of your hand; if hot, use another
  way. Have two exits from every space in your plan.
STARTING FIRE: gather tinder (dry, fluffy), kindling (pencil to thumb), fuel
(bigger). Shelter the flame from wind, build small, add air, feed gradually.
Keep a fire small, contained, and downwind of your shelter.

=== 7. FIRST AID: LIFE THREATS FIRST ===
Principle: fix what kills fastest, in order, and get to professional care as
soon as possible. This manual gives principles and escalation, not doses.
CHECK: Are they responsive? Breathing? Bleeding badly? Approach only if the
scene is safe.
NOT BREATHING / NO PULSE: start CPR. Push hard and fast in the center of the
chest, about 100-120/min, ~5-6 cm deep, let it recoil; minimize pauses. If
trained and willing, add rescue breaths; if not, hands-only is far better than
nothing. Use an AED if available and follow its voice.
CHOKING (cannot cough/speak/breathe): 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades,
then 5 abdominal thrusts (inward-upward above the navel); alternate. Infant: 5
back blows then 5 chest thrusts, head low. If they collapse, start CPR.
SEVERE BLEEDING: press hard directly on the wound with cloth; keep pressing
(10+ min) and add layers, do not peek. If a limb bleeds uncontrollably, apply a
tourniquet 5-7 cm above the wound (not on a joint), tighten until bleeding
stops, note the time, do not loosen. Pack deep wounds with cloth and press.
SHOCK (pale, cold, clammy, fast weak pulse, confusion): lay flat, keep warm,
reassure, treat causes (bleeding), sips of water only if fully alert and no
belly injury. Escalate.
BURNS: stop the burning; cool with clean cool (not ice) water 20 min; remove
non-stuck jewelry/clothing; cover loosely clean/non-fluffy. Do not pop blisters
or apply grease. Large, deep, airway, face, hands, genitals, or electrical/
chemical burns: escalate urgently. Chemical: brush off dry powder, flush with
water long.
FRACTURES/SPRAINS: do not straighten obvious deformities; immobilize/splint in
found position padding above and below the joint; check the limb stays warm and
pink (circulation). Open fracture: cover wound, control bleeding, escalate.
HEAD/SPINE injury: if suspected, keep them still, support the head in line, do
not move unless in danger; watch breathing.
UNCONSCIOUS but breathing: place on their side (recovery position) to protect
the airway; monitor.
MEDICATIONS: prefer principle over precise dosing you are unsure of. Do not
guess unfamiliar drug doses. Keep chronic medicines going if you can; ration and
protect insulin/inhalers/heart/seizure meds; seek resupply early. For pain/fever
use only medicines the person already tolerates, at labeled adult/child doses;
never exceed the label; avoid giving aspirin to children. When unsure, wait and
escalate rather than risk harm.
WOUND CARE over days: clean hands, irrigate with safe water, keep covered and
dry, watch for infection (spreading redness, heat, pus, red streaks, fever) -
infection is the slow killer; escalate for antibiotics if these appear.

=== 8. ILLNESS, HYGIENE, SANITATION ===
More people die after disasters from disease and dirty water than from the
event. Hygiene is survival.
HANDS: wash with soap and safe water before food/medical and after toilet/waste;
use ash or sanitizer if no soap.
HUMAN WASTE: keep it far (at least ~30 m / 100 ft and downhill) from water
sources, kitchens, and living space. Options: existing toilet with manual
flush (pour water), bucket toilet with lid and bags plus cover material
(sawdust, soil, lime) to control smell and flies, or a cathole 15-20 cm deep
buried after. Never contaminate drinking water.
DIARRHEA/VOMITING is dangerous through dehydration, especially for kids and
elders. Treat with frequent small sips of oral rehydration: roughly 6 level
teaspoons sugar + half a level teaspoon salt in 1 liter safe water; keep giving.
Escalate for bloody stool, high fever, severe dehydration (sunken eyes, no
urine, lethargy).
GARBAGE and bodies: separate and cover waste; manage the deceased with respect,
away from water and food, documented, until authorities can respond.
PESTS: control flies and mosquitoes (cover water, drain standing water, screens)
- they spread disease.

=== 9. POWER, LIGHT, FUEL ===
Conserve first. Turn off nonessential loads. Protect the ability to charge a
phone/radio.
PHONE: dim screen, airplane mode when no signal, close apps, keep it warm, one
device on at a time; text uses less power/bandwidth than calls.
BATTERIES: keep spares; store cool; do not mix old/new or types; watch for
leaks. Car battery + inverter can run small loads; do not drain the starting
battery flat.
GENERATORS: OUTSIDE only (see CO). Never backfeed into house wiring without a
proper transfer switch - it can kill line workers and you. Refuel cold/off.
FUEL: store approved containers, ventilated, away from living space and
ignition; label and date; stabilize gasoline; keep clear of heat.
LIGHT: headlamps free hands; ration; candles are a fire risk - never leave lit
or near fabric/sleep.

=== 10. COMMUNICATIONS AND SIGNALING ===
Information and coordination save lives; so does being found.
MONITOR: a battery/hand-crank/solar radio for official broadcasts (AM/FM,
NOAA weather radio in the US). Follow official evacuation and boil-water orders.
FAMILY PLAN: agree in advance on an out-of-area contact (often reachable when
local lines are jammed), two meeting points (near home, outside neighborhood),
and that TEXT often works when calls fail. Write numbers on paper.
TWO-WAY RADIO: FRS/GMRS/CB for short range; set a channel and check-in times to
save battery. Ham radio reaches far if licensed operators are present.
SIGNAL FOR RESCUE: THREE of anything = distress (3 whistle blasts, 3 fires in a
triangle, 3 flashes). A whistle beats your voice and lasts longer. Use a mirror/
shiny object to flash aircraft. Make large ground signals with contrast: V =
need assistance, X = need medical help, arrow = direction traveled. Keep a
signal ready; you may get one chance.

=== 11. STAY OR GO: EVACUATION AND NAVIGATION ===
DECIDE deliberately. STAY if your shelter is safe, you have water/warmth, and
leaving is more dangerous (e.g., told to shelter in place, or roads are deadly).
GO if there is fire, flood, structural, gas, or violence threat, if officials
order evacuation, or if staying means running out of water/heat with no resupply.
BEFORE LEAVING: grab your go-bag, wear sturdy shoes and layers, fill water, tell
someone your route and destination, leave a note (who, where, when, condition),
shut off utilities if appropriate, take documents and medicines.
ROUTES: prefer official routes; avoid flooded roads - "Turn Around, Don't Drown":
even 15 cm of moving water can knock you down and 30 cm can float a car. Avoid
low ground in floods, high open ground in lightning, and downwind of fire/chem.
NAVIGATION: keep a paper map and compass. Sun rises roughly east, sets west; at
solar noon shadows point away from the sun (toward true north in the northern
hemisphere, south in the southern). Note landmarks; mark your trail; travel in
daylight; do not travel exhausted or in whiteout.
VEHICLE: keep the tank at least half full pre-crisis; carry water, blankets,
jumper cables, basic tools; if stranded in cold, run engine only briefly and
only with a clear exhaust and a cracked window (CO), stay with the vehicle - it
is shelter and easier to find.

=== 12. PEOPLE: GROUPS, SECURITY, VULNERABLE MEMBERS ===
Cooperation outperforms isolation. Organize: shared tasks, clear decisions,
fair rationing, and watch shifts. Calm leadership and routine reduce panic.
SECURITY: lock/reinforce as reasonable; keep low profile (light/noise/odor
discipline) if others may be hostile; avoid displaying supplies. De-escalate
conflict; distance and negotiation beat confrontation. Protect children from
hazards and from wandering.
VULNERABLE MEMBERS: plan for infants (warmth, feeding, safe water), elders and
the ill (medications, mobility, temperature), people with disabilities (assistive
devices, communication needs), and pets (water, restraint, waste). Keep families
together; assign a buddy to each dependent person.
MENTAL HEALTH: expect fear, grief, and stress reactions; they are normal.
Maintain routines, talk, share tasks, rest, and watch for despair or reckless
behavior. Purpose and connection keep people alive.

=== 13. DOCUMENTS, MONEY, RECORDS ===
Keep waterproof copies (paper and/or offline digital) of ID, insurance, medical
info and medication lists, contacts, property and financial records, and
photos of home/valuables. Keep some cash in small bills - electronic payment
may be down. Record decisions, injuries, and who went where; good records speed
recovery, reunification, and aid/insurance claims.

=== 14. SCENARIO QUICK-ACTIONS ===
EARTHQUAKE: Drop, Cover, Hold On under sturdy furniture; stay away from windows;
if in bed, stay and cover your head; do not run outside during shaking. After:
check for injuries/gas/fire, expect aftershocks, get to open ground if the
building is damaged.
FIRE (structure/wildfire): get out and stay out; crawl low under smoke; wildfire
- evacuate early, close windows, remove flammables near the house, wear
non-synthetic clothing, go upwind/uphill away from the fire's path.
FLOOD: move to higher ground before water rises; never walk/drive through
floodwater; assume it is contaminated and electrified; boil-water rules after.
TORNADO: go to a small interior room/basement on the lowest floor, cover your
head and neck; a vehicle/mobile home is unsafe - get to sturdy shelter or lie
in a low ditch as last resort.
HURRICANE/CYCLONE: prepare days ahead, evacuate if told, shelter interior away
from windows during the storm, beware the calm eye, expect flooding and long
outages after.
WINTER STORM: stay in, conserve heat to one room, prevent CO, layer, keep pipes
from freezing (drip), watch for hypothermia.
EXTREME HEAT: stay cool, hydrate, never leave people/pets in vehicles, check on
elders, reduce activity to early/late hours.
POWER OUTAGE: conserve phone, keep fridge/freezer closed, no generators indoors,
watch for CO and food spoilage, check on neighbors.
PANDEMIC/DISEASE: distance, hygiene, ventilate, isolate the sick, protect the
vulnerable, follow official guidance.
CHEMICAL/HAZMAT: move upwind and uphill, get inside and seal (shelter-in-place)
or evacuate as ordered, remove and bag contaminated clothing, rinse skin/eyes
with water.
NUCLEAR/RADIATION or "dirty" event: get inside a sturdy building, go to the
center/basement, stay for at least 24 h unless told otherwise (Get Inside, Stay
Inside, Stay Tuned), remove outer clothing and wash to remove fallout.

=== 15. LONGER-TERM RECOVERY ===
When immediate threats pass, shift from surviving to sustaining.
WATER: secure a renewable source (rain catchment off clean roofs, protected
well/spring) plus ongoing disinfection and safe storage.
FOOD: transition to production - garden from saved seed, preserve harvests
(drying, salting, fermenting, canning if equipped), and protect stores from
pests and damp. Learn a few reliable local calorie crops.
SHELTER/ENERGY: weatherproof and repair; add insulation; develop safe heating/
cooking with proper ventilation; small solar/wind if available.
HEALTH: maintain sanitation discipline, wound care, and medication supply;
build a simple sick-care routine and isolation plan.
COMMUNITY: pool skills and labor, trade fairly, share knowledge, care for the
vulnerable, and rebuild governance and routine. Skills, tools, seeds, and
trust are the real long-term wealth. Teach others; redundancy of knowledge is
survival.

=== 16. KITS AND CHECKLISTS ===
GO-BAG (per person, grab-and-run): water + filter/tablets, high-calorie food,
weather-appropriate clothing and rain shell, emergency blanket, sturdy shoes,
flashlight/headlamp + batteries, whistle, multitool/knife, lighter/matches +
tinder, first-aid basics, personal medications, N95/dust mask, gloves, phone +
backup battery, paper map, cash, copies of documents, hygiene items, duct tape,
cordage, and a written contact/plan card.
HOME (shelter-in-place): 2 weeks water (or the means to disinfect), 2+ weeks
food, sanitation supplies (bags, cover material, lime), battery/crank radio,
CO alarm, fire extinguisher, extended first aid and medications, tools to shut
off utilities, lighting and fuel stored safely, warm bedding.
SKILLS beat gear: water disinfection, fire, first aid/CPR, navigation, basic
repair, food preservation, and calm decision-making. Practice before you need
them. Review and rotate supplies on a schedule.

REMEMBER: protect life first, then capability. Fix the fastest killer in front
of you, avoid becoming a casualty, keep your people together, and get to real
help as soon as it exists. Three of anything means help.
