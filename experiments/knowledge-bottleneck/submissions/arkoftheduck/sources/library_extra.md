ARKOFTHEDUCK EXTENDED LIBRARY (1M TIER, AUTHORED LAYER)
This layer appears only at the largest budget. It assumes the reader has time
to build capability, not just survive the first hours. Same rule as before:
principle and escalation over false precision; real professionals and official
guidance always take priority.

=== A. WATER SYSTEMS FOR THE LONG HAUL ===
Move from one-off disinfection to a dependable supply.
- Rainwater: catch off a clean, non-toxic roof (avoid asphalt-shingle runoff for
  drinking if possible; never lead flashing or treated wood). First-flush divert
  the initial dirty flow. Screen inlets against mosquitoes and debris. Store in
  opaque, covered, food-safe tanks; keep light out to stop algae. Disinfect
  before drinking regardless of source.
- Wells/springs: protect the head from surface runoff and animals; test if you
  ever can; shock-disinfect a contaminated well with chlorine and pump through.
- Filtration ladder: settle -> cloth -> sand/charcoal biofilter (for clarity and
  some pathogens/taste) -> disinfect (boil/chlorine/UV). Clarity first always
  improves disinfection.
- Chemical/salt problems: filtration and boiling do NOT remove salt, heavy
  metals, or many industrial chemicals. Distillation (boil, condense the steam
  on a cool surface, collect) removes salt and many contaminants but is slow and
  fuel-hungry; use it only when the only water is salty/brackish or chemically
  suspect and no clean source exists.
- Daily discipline: keep "raw" and "treated" containers strictly separate and
  labeled; never dip dirty hands/cups into stored treated water; use a spigot or
  pour.

=== B. FOOD: STORAGE, PRESERVATION, PRODUCTION ===
- Storage principles: cool, dark, dry, sealed, pest-proof; rotate oldest first;
  protect from rodents and moisture; date everything. Fats/oils go rancid -
  store cool and use first. Dry staples (grains, beans, rice, oats, pasta,
  sugar, salt) last years if kept dry and sealed.
- Preservation methods (learn at least two):
  * Drying: the oldest and most forgiving. Thin slices, low steady heat or dry
    airflow, until brittle/leathery. Great for fruit, vegetables, herbs, and
    lean meat (jerky) - keep meat drying hot enough and fast to avoid spoilage.
  * Salting/curing: salt draws out water and inhibits microbes; traditional for
    meat and fish; requires enough salt and cool conditions.
  * Fermenting: salt brine or natural lacto-fermentation (sauerkraut, pickles)
    preserves vegetables and adds nutrients; keep submerged under brine, cool.
  * Canning: powerful but unforgiving. High-acid foods can be water-bath canned;
    low-acid foods (most vegetables, meat) REQUIRE a pressure canner to prevent
    botulism - do not improvise this. If a jar is unsealed, leaking, foamy, or
    smells off, discard without tasting. When unsure, do not can low-acid foods.
  * Root cellaring: cool, humid, dark storage for roots, squash, cabbage,
    apples; keep apples separate (they hasten spoilage of others).
- Production: start a garden from saved open-pollinated (not hybrid/F1) seed so
  you can save seed again. Prioritize dense-calorie, reliable crops for your
  climate (potatoes, beans, squash, corn, roots, leafy greens for vitamins).
  Save seed from the best plants; dry and store cool. Compost waste to rebuild
  soil. Protect crops from pests, frost, and drought. Small livestock (chickens,
  rabbits) convert scraps to protein if you can secure and feed them.
- Foraging caution: never eat a wild plant/mushroom you cannot identify with
  certainty; many look-alikes are deadly; when uncertain, do not eat it.

=== C. EXTENDED MEDICAL CAPABILITY (PRINCIPLES) ===
This is not a substitute for training or clinicians. Get real first-aid/CPR
training before a crisis if you can.
- Assessment routine: scene safe -> responsive? -> airway -> breathing ->
  circulation (bleeding) -> then head-to-toe check and history. Reassess often.
- Infection is the long-term killer. Prevent it: clean hands, irrigate wounds
  with plenty of safe water, remove debris, keep wounds clean, dry, covered;
  leave grossly contaminated or animal/human-bite wounds open and escalate.
  Watch for spreading redness, warmth, swelling, pus, red streaks, fever - these
  mean serious infection needing antibiotics and professional care; escalate.
- Dehydration and diarrheal disease kill quietly, especially children and
  elders: oral rehydration solution (about 6 tsp sugar + 0.5 tsp salt per liter
  of safe water), given as frequent small sips, is one of the highest-value
  interventions that exists. Continue feeding as tolerated.
- Chronic conditions: protect and ration essential medicines (insulin, heart,
  seizure, asthma, psychiatric); seek resupply early; know which cannot be
  stopped abruptly. Keep an updated medication and allergy list for each person.
- Pain/fever: use only medicines the person tolerates, at labeled doses; never
  exceed the label; avoid aspirin in children; be cautious combining products
  that share ingredients. When in doubt, less is safer; escalate.
- Childbirth, major trauma, chest pain, stroke signs (face droop, arm weakness,
  speech trouble), difficulty breathing, severe allergic reaction: these are
  beyond improvisation - provide basic support (position, warmth, reassurance,
  bleeding control, CPR if needed) and get professional help by any means.
- Dental/eye/burn care and splinting can be improvised on sound principles
  (clean, immobilize, protect, escalate) but do not attempt procedures you do
  not understand; stabilizing and transporting beats a risky field attempt.

=== D. SHELTER, HEAT, AND REPAIR ===
- Weatherproofing: stop water intrusion first (roof, then walls, then ground),
  then wind, then add insulation. Trapped dead-air space is insulation - layers,
  gaps, and loft matter more than thickness of any single material.
- Improvised insulation: leaves, straw, crumpled paper, textiles, bubble wrap,
  foam. Insulate the floor/ground and the ceiling first (heat rises and sinks
  fastest there).
- Safe heating: any combustion needs an outside air path and an exhaust path -
  vent it, use a CO alarm, never sleep with an unvented flame. A single small
  heated room is easier to keep warm than a whole home. Radiant heat (masonry,
  water containers) holds warmth after a fire dies.
- Basic repair: learn to patch a roof/tarp, board a window, stop a leak (shut
  water main, then fix), and make safe temporary structural braces. Duct tape,
  cordage, plastic sheeting, screws, and a few hand tools solve a huge fraction
  of problems.

=== E. POWER AND LIGHT, DEEPER ===
- Budget energy like water: list loads, cut all nonessentials, power only what
  preserves life and information (light, radio, charging, refrigeration of
  medicine).
- Small solar: a panel + charge controller + battery + inverter can sustain
  phones, radios, lights, and small loads; size the battery to your night needs;
  keep panels clean and angled to the sun; protect batteries from deep discharge
  and freezing.
- Safety: never backfeed a generator into house wiring without a transfer
  switch; fuse/breaker your circuits; keep batteries ventilated (they can vent
  explosive gas); match voltages; insulate tools when working near stored energy.

=== F. COMMUNICATIONS, DEEPER ===
- Layered comms plan: (1) monitor official broadcasts on a battery/crank radio;
  (2) short-range family/team radios with agreed channels and scheduled check-in
  windows to save battery; (3) an out-of-area contact as a message relay; (4)
  written fallback (notes at meeting points). Text/SMS often survives when calls
  fail.
- Signaling for rescue is a discipline: keep signals pre-staged (whistle on you,
  fuel for a signal fire, a mirror). Three evenly spaced signals = distress.
  Ground-to-air: large, high-contrast symbols in the open (V need help, X need
  medical, an arrow for direction). Movement and contrast attract the eye; a
  steady mirror flash is visible for miles.

=== G. SECURITY AND SOCIAL ORDER ===
- The goal is deterrence and avoidance, not confrontation. Reduce your signature
  (light, noise, smell, visible supplies). Control access; keep a watch rotation
  for groups. Cache critical supplies redundantly.
- Conflict: distance, calm voice, and negotiation resolve most encounters; never
  escalate over replaceable things. Protect people first.
- Fairness prevents collapse: transparent rationing, shared work, and clear,
  humane rules keep a group functioning. Injustice and hoarding destroy groups
  faster than any outside threat.

=== H. PSYCHOLOGY AND ENDURANCE ===
- Expect acute stress: racing heart, tunnel vision, shaking, numbness - normal;
  slow breathing (in 4, hold 4, out 4) restores function. Give people simple,
  concrete tasks; action reduces panic.
- Sustain morale: routine, roles, rest, real food/water, connection, small
  wins, and meaning. Watch for withdrawal, hopelessness, reckless or aggressive
  behavior, and grief; talk, include, and share the load. Sleep and hydration
  are cognitive medicine - a rested, hydrated group makes fewer fatal mistakes.
- Children need reassurance, routine, honesty at their level, and a job; keep
  them close and occupied.

=== I. NAVIGATION AND TRAVEL, DEEPER ===
- Carry a paper map and compass and know your declination. Without a compass:
  the sun rises broadly east and sets west; a shadow-stick's shortest shadow
  points true north/south at solar noon; stars (the celestial pole) give a fixed
  reference at night. These are rough - use terrain, water flow (downhill leads
  toward larger water and often people), and known landmarks.
- Travel smart: daylight only, pace to the weakest member, ration water, avoid
  crossing dangerous water and exposed ridgelines in storms, mark your route,
  and tell others your plan. Being still and findable often beats moving.

=== J. TOOLS, KNOTS, AND FIXES ===
- A few knots solve most needs: a loop that won't slip (bowline), a binding that
  holds and releases (round turn and two half hitches), a shortening/anchor
  (clove hitch), and a join for two ropes (sheet bend). Practice until automatic.
- Priority tools: knife/multitool, cordage, duct tape, tarp/plastic sheeting,
  fire-making, container/pot, needle and thread, wire, and a means to dig. With
  these and knowledge you can improvise most immediate needs.
- Maintain gear: dry it, sharpen it, protect from rust and grit; a working tool
  is worth many broken ones. Redundancy for the few truly critical items (fire,
  water treatment, light) is worth the weight.

=== K. REBUILDING AND KNOWLEDGE PRESERVATION ===
- The endgame is restored capability: reliable water and food, safe shelter and
  heat, health and sanitation, communication, and cooperative governance.
- Preserve knowledge deliberately: write down what works, teach others, and keep
  durable copies of critical procedures. A skill known by only one person is a
  single point of failure. Redundancy of knowledge across people is the deepest
  form of preparedness - which is exactly why an artifact like this exists.
- Trade and community: fair exchange of surplus, pooled specialties, and mutual
  aid rebuild an economy and morale together. Trust, skills, tools, and seeds
  are the real wealth after infrastructure fails.
